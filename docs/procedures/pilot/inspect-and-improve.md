# PROC-PILOT-007: Inspect Failure and Friction

## Purpose

Convert failures, overrides, support demand and repeated friction into owned improvements to requirements, context, harness, workflow, controls, tools or readiness.

## Use when

A review cadence, incident, repeated support event, override, failure cluster or participant concern triggers inspection.

## Do not use when

Active containment takes priority or evidence is used to rank or punish individual workers.

## Accountable owner

ROLE-OPS-015 pilot support and learning owner.

## Participants

- Participants, delivery and pilot owners.
- Context, platform, control, security, service and measurement owners.
- Incident and workforce representatives as applicable.

## Preconditions

- Relevant events and source evidence are preserved.
- Participants can report safely.
- Unsafe scope is already restricted pending inspection.

## Inputs

- Failures, stops, overrides, exceptions, incidents and near misses.
- Support events, participant feedback and procedure evidence.
- Context, plan, control, tool and environment versions.

## Actions

1. **The learning owner assembles events.** Include successful work for comparison and preserve severity and chronology.
2. **The group validates facts.** Separate observed events, inference, unknowns and affected scope.
3. **The group classifies contributing systems.** Assess requirement, context, harness, workflow, control, tool, model, environment, ownership, training, workload and incentive causes.
4. **Owners identify control and authority implications.** Keep unsafe work restricted and expand containment if impact is uncertain.
5. **The group distinguishes one-off and repeated patterns.** Link recurrence across teams, procedures and versions without blaming users for system defects.
6. **The applicable system owner creates corrective action.** State change, validation evidence, owner, due date, affected work and deployment plan.
7. **Control owners test the improvement.** Repeat the failed scenario plus representative allowed and prohibited scenarios.
8. **Governance decides re-run or continued restriction.** Require new classification, readiness or grant where material.
9. **The learning owner publishes bounded lessons.** Update canonical context, controls or procedures and preserve applicability limits.

## Required human decisions

- Incident and control owners decide safety and restriction.
- Each system-element owner accepts root-cause and remediation accountability.
- Governance decides restoration or rerun.
- AI may cluster evidence but cannot assign blame or declare root cause.

## Outputs

- Validated event and pattern record.
- Multi-factor cause classification.
- Immediate restriction and containment decisions.
- Owned corrective actions with validation.
- Updated canonical system element and restoration decision.

## Verification

- Analysis considers more than prompt quality.
- Repeated patterns produce system changes.
- Unsafe work remains technically restricted.
- Corrective action passes the original and negative scenarios.
- Updated guidance states scope and version.

## Stop conditions

Keep work stopped when impact is unknown, evidence is unreliable, recovery or revocation failed, remediation lacks an owner, retest fails or participant reporting is unsafe.

## Completion criteria

- Causes, limits and evidence are durable.
- Each repeated pattern has an owned system response.
- Restrictions and restored grants match governance decisions.
- Improvement is verified before similar work resumes.

## Measures

Record recurrence, cause distribution, time to restriction and remediation, validation failures, support reduction and defects escaping after claimed improvement.
