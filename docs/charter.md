# Project Charter

## Purpose

Create a vendor-neutral, executable enterprise playbook for introducing, governing, validating and progressively expanding AI-assisted software delivery.

The playbook must enable organizations to begin with limited assistance, learn from real delivery work, strengthen their engineering system and grant additional authority only where evidence supports it.

## Problem

Most available guidance is fragmented. Organizations can find lifecycle concepts, adoption research, specification methods, context practices, security controls and product rollout guidance, but not one coherent implementation system.

Enterprises need guidance that connects:

- Organizational readiness
- Delivery workflow
- Durable context and artifacts
- Human accountability
- Bounded agent authority
- Engineering controls
- Production feedback
- Measurement and advancement decisions

## Intended users

- Technology executives
- Engineering leaders
- Enterprise architects
- Platform engineering and developer-experience teams
- Product and delivery leaders
- Security, risk, legal and compliance teams
- Software delivery teams
- Site reliability and operations teams
- Transformation and enablement leaders

## Product outcome

An enterprise can use the playbook to:

1. Determine where AI assistance is appropriate.
2. Establish the minimum organizational and technical foundation.
3. Run bounded pilots using real delivery work.
4. Execute repeatable procedures with defined roles, artifacts and gates.
5. Measure delivery-system outcomes and risks.
6. Expand, restrict or revoke AI authority based on evidence.
7. Operate different adoption states across teams without fragmenting enterprise governance.

## In scope

- Normative principles and requirements
- Progressive adoption states
- Readiness assessment and remediation
- Enterprise and team operating responsibilities
- Change-risk classification
- Human and AI responsibilities
- Procedures for assisted work, bounded changes and governed workflows
- Context, harness and control requirements
- Required artifacts and evidence
- Pilot, measurement and advancement practices
- Worked enterprise scenarios
- Facilitation and adoption guidance
- Governance, exceptions, incidents and periodic reassessment

## Out of scope

- Product-specific setup instructions
- Vendor comparisons or purchasing recommendations
- Model benchmarking
- Building AI-enabled products for end users
- General AI governance unrelated to software delivery
- Claims that all teams should progress to autonomous delivery
- Replacement of accountable human roles by AI systems

## Assumed best-known foundation

The playbook synthesizes:

- DORA readiness and delivery capabilities
- AWS AI-DLC lifecycle concepts
- GitHub Spec Kit artifact discipline
- Thoughtworks context and harness engineering practices
- Optiver's agent-oriented delivery substrate lessons
- OpenSSF and FINOS security and tool-governance controls
- A measured, pilot-led enterprise rollout pattern

These are inputs, not authorities that override the playbook's own documented decisions.

## Success criteria

The product is successful when a qualified enterprise team can execute its procedures without hidden author context and can produce auditable evidence for readiness, delivery, control and advancement decisions.
