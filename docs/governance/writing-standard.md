# Writing Standard

## Normative language

- **Must** indicates a mandatory requirement.
- **Must not** indicates a prohibition.
- **Should** indicates a recommended practice that may be varied with documented reasoning.
- **May** indicates an allowed option.

Do not use normative language for unsupported preferences.

## Procedure schema

Every executable procedure must contain:

1. **Identifier and name**
2. **Purpose**
3. **Use when**
4. **Do not use when**
5. **Accountable owner**
6. **Participants**
7. **Preconditions**
8. **Inputs**
9. **Actions**
10. **Required human decisions**
11. **Outputs**
12. **Verification**
13. **Stop conditions**
14. **Completion criteria**
15. **Measures**
16. **Next procedures**
17. **Applicable principles and controls**

Actions must identify who acts, what is done and what evidence is recorded.

## Control schema

Every control must define:

- Identifier and name
- Purpose
- Control type
- Applicable adoption states and risks
- Owner
- Enforcement or evaluation mechanism
- Evidence
- Failure response
- Review frequency

## Artifact schema

Every artifact must define:

- Identifier and name
- Purpose
- When required
- Owner
- Required content
- Producer and approver
- Storage and retention expectations
- Consumers
- Validation
- Review or expiry conditions

## Content quality

- Write for execution, not inspiration.
- Use short sections and mobile-readable lists.
- Avoid wide tables.
- Avoid decorative diagrams.
- Separate fact, interpretation and project decision.
- Define an observable verification method for every mandatory statement.
- Provide filled examples for artifacts whose acceptable quality may otherwise be ambiguous.
