# Complete the Pilot Charter

## Why am I here?

Bring every preparation decision into one approved agreement that tells the team what may start.

**Owner:** Pilot lead  
**Participants:** Sponsor and every named decision owner  
**Time:** 60 minutes after preparation is complete  
**Bring:** Ownership, use-case, readiness, boundary and baseline decisions  
**Leave with:** An approved pilot charter or a clear list of unresolved blockers

## Complete the charter

The charter must state:

- pilot intent and decision to be informed;
- sponsor, pilot lead, participants and decision rights;
- selected team, product context and use cases;
- operating mode for each use case;
- explicit exclusions;
- start and end conditions;
- approved tools, repositories, data and environments;
- authority and access limits;
- required engineering, security, verification, review and release controls;
- baseline and measures;
- weekly operating and escalation rhythm;
- stop, incident, revocation and recovery conditions;
- final decision date and decision owner.

## Review it from the team's perspective

Ask a participating team member to explain:

- what work is allowed;
- what AI may do;
- what the person must still do;
- when work must stop;
- where support comes from;
- what evidence the team is expected to capture.

If the answer requires reading several control documents, simplify the team-facing wording while keeping the canonical requirements intact.

## Approve the exact version

Record who approved the charter, the date and the exact version. A material change to use case, authority, data, tooling, control or success criteria requires reassessment and approval. Silence is not approval.

## Stop here if

Any prerequisite is incomplete, an approver has not accepted their accountability, the team cannot explain the boundary, or the charter relies on a control that has not been tested.
