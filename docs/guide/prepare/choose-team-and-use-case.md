# Choose the Team and Use Case

## Why am I here?

Select a pilot that is real enough to teach you something and bounded enough to control.

**Owner:** Pilot lead  
**Participants:** Engineering manager, product owner, delivery team, platform and risk partners  
**Time:** 60 to 90 minutes  
**Bring:** Candidate teams, candidate work and named decision owners  
**Leave with:** One selected team, one primary use case and documented exclusions

## Choose the team

Prefer a team that:

- has stable membership and a supportive manager;
- can run its normal build, test, review and release process;
- understands its product and repositories;
- delivers often enough to produce repeated observations;
- is willing to report problems and uncertainty;
- has capacity for onboarding, weekly review and evidence capture.

Do not choose a team because it is already overwhelmed, politically visible or expected to prove a predetermined result.

## Choose the work

For a first pilot, prefer repeatable, reviewable work with clear inputs and acceptance criteria. Examples include explaining an unfamiliar component, analyzing requirements, drafting delivery documentation, suggesting tests, assisting code review, or correcting a bounded low-risk defect.

Avoid:

- emergency or critical-path work;
- material production changes;
- poorly understood legacy areas without a reliable baseline;
- work requiring broad secrets or sensitive data;
- changes with unclear ownership or acceptance criteria;
- work that cannot be reversed or independently checked.

## Make the selection

1. Describe the user or business need.
2. Define the work type and expected frequency.
3. State why AI assistance may help.
4. Identify affected systems, repositories, data and people.
5. Identify the worst credible failure and how it would be detected.
6. State what is explicitly out of scope.
7. Choose Assisted Work unless Bounded Change is necessary and separately justified.
8. Record the decision and rejected alternatives.

## Stop here if

The use case is valuable only if controls are bypassed, requires authority the organization cannot contain, or cannot generate comparable evidence during the pilot period.
