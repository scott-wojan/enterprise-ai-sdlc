# What the Default Pilot Looks Like

## Why am I here?

Use this page to align everyone on the starting shape of the pilot before debating tools or advanced automation.

**Owner:** Pilot lead  
**Participants:** Sponsor, engineering manager, delivery team, platform and risk partners  
**Time:** 30 minutes  
**Bring:** The pilot intent and quick-screen decision  
**Leave with:** Agreement on the default pilot shape and any proposed deviation

## Start small enough to learn

The default pilot uses:

- one stable delivery team;
- one bounded product or service context;
- one or two suitable use cases;
- a fixed pilot period long enough to observe repeated work;
- existing engineering and release controls;
- explicit access and authority boundaries;
- weekly review of outcomes, failures and team experience;
- a final human decision to continue, restrict, expand or stop.

The exact duration depends on the team's delivery cadence. Choose a period that produces several comparable work items without turning the pilot into a permanent exception.

## Begin with Assisted Work

In Assisted Work, AI may explain, analyze, draft, suggest or review. A person applies every change and remains responsible for checking the result. This is the normal starting point because it lets the organization learn about context quality, output reliability, workflow fit and team behavior without granting execution authority.

## Add Bounded Change only when justified

Bounded Change permits AI to modify an isolated working copy within an approved plan and tightly enforced limits. It requires stronger readiness, technical containment, deterministic verification, independent human review, recoverability and evidence.

Do not add Bounded Change merely because the tool supports it. Add it only for eligible work after the pilot's decision group confirms that the additional authority is necessary and controlled.

## Keep enterprise controls intact

The pilot does not create a shortcut around source control, testing, peer review, security checks, approvals, release management or incident response. AI-generated work follows the same release path as human-generated work. Additional controls may apply because AI introduces different failure modes.

## What the pilot is trying to learn

The pilot tests whether the operating system works, not whether a model can produce impressive output. Examine delivery flow, quality, operational effects, security and control behavior, product outcomes, participant experience and economic cost together.

## Stop here if

The proposed pilot depends on production write access, unrestricted secrets, unreviewed releases, self-approval, critical emergency work or a team that cannot reproduce its current build and test baseline.

Detailed operating states are defined in [Assisted Work](../../adoption/assisted-work.md) and [Bounded Change](../../adoption/bounded-change.md).
