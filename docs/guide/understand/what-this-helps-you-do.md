# What This Playbook Helps You Do

## Why am I here?

Use this page to confirm that the playbook solves the problem your organization actually has before investing in a pilot.

**Owner:** Executive sponsor and pilot lead  
**Participants:** Engineering, platform, security or risk, and delivery leadership  
**Time:** 20 minutes  
**Bring:** The business reason for considering AI-assisted delivery  
**Leave with:** A one-sentence pilot intent, or a decision not to proceed

## The problem it addresses

Many enterprises have individual developers using AI, but no shared answer to basic operating questions:

- Which work is appropriate?
- Who remains accountable?
- What information and tools may AI access?
- How will a proposed or completed change be checked?
- What evidence will show whether the pilot helped?
- What happens when output is wrong, access is excessive or the team loses confidence?
- Who decides whether to continue?

This playbook turns those questions into a controlled pilot that a real team can run.

## What you should expect to accomplish

By the end of the pilot, the organization should be able to say:

- what kind of AI-assisted work was attempted;
- why the selected team and use case were suitable;
- which boundaries and controls governed the work;
- what changed in delivery, quality, risk and team experience;
- what failed or created friction;
- whether the same operating conditions should continue, change or stop.

The pilot is successful when it produces a trustworthy decision. That decision may be to stop. A pilot that exposes an unsafe assumption before broad adoption has created value.

## Write the pilot intent

Complete this sentence:

> We are considering a controlled pilot to learn whether AI-assisted delivery can ______ for ______, without degrading ______.

Good intents name an outcome, a bounded group and an important constraint. Avoid intents such as “use AI everywhere,” “increase developer productivity” or “become agentic.” They are too broad to guide a pilot or support a defensible decision.

## Stop here if

- Leadership wants broad deployment before evidence exists.
- No human owner will be accountable for pilot decisions.
- The organization intends to bypass existing engineering, security or release controls.
- The only desired outcome is a predetermined success story.

Resolve those conditions before proceeding.
