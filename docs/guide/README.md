# Start Here

This playbook helps an enterprise run a small, controlled AI-assisted software-delivery pilot, learn whether it improves delivery, and decide what to do next.

It is for consulting teams and enterprise leaders who need something more rigorous than individual coding experiments. It is vendor-neutral and designed for gradual adoption. The goal is not to make software delivery fully autonomous. The goal is to learn where AI helps, under what conditions, with what controls, and with accountable people making the decisions.

## Your first action

Name one person as the **pilot lead**. Give that person authority to coordinate the pilot, bring the required participants together, and stop work when the agreed conditions are not met.

The pilot lead should read the five Understand pages, then begin Prepare. Do not begin with tool selection.

## What happens in the pilot

1. **Understand:** agree on the purpose, shape and participants.
2. **Prepare:** choose one suitable team and use case, assess readiness, set boundaries, establish a baseline and approve a charter.
3. **Run:** onboard the team, use AI only within the approved mode, review progress weekly and capture lightweight evidence.
4. **Decide:** review evidence and choose to continue, restrict, expand or stop.
5. **Reference:** use one Pilot Workbook for the work, with examples and detailed source material available when needed.

## Who needs to be involved

At minimum, involve an executive sponsor, pilot lead, engineering manager, participating delivery team, platform or enablement lead, security or risk partner, and measurement owner. One person may cover more than one role in a smaller organization, but every decision must still have a named human owner.

## What this guide does not do

- It does not approve a vendor or tool.
- It does not authorize AI to make consequential decisions.
- It does not replace existing security, engineering, release or compliance controls.
- It does not treat speed or code volume as proof of value.
- It does not assume every team or type of work should advance to greater AI authority.
