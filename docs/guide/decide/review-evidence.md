# Review the Pilot Evidence

## Why am I here?

Turn the pilot record into a balanced explanation of what changed, what caused it and how confident the organization should be.

**Owner:** Measurement owner  
**Participants:** Pilot lead, engineering manager, product, delivery representatives, platform, quality, operations, security or risk and support owners  
**Time:** 90 minutes plus preparation  
**Bring:** Baseline, weekly records, accepted work, failures, incidents, costs and participant feedback  
**Leave with:** A shared evidence summary, unresolved uncertainties and decision constraints

## Prepare the evidence

Use the same definitions and sources established before launch. Separate:

- observations from interpretation;
- accepted work from generated output;
- local improvement from transferred effort;
- isolated events from recurring patterns;
- AI contribution from unrelated changes in staffing, scope or delivery conditions.

Do not discard negative evidence because the pilot produced a visible success. Do not discard a real benefit because another measure stayed flat.

## Review each outcome area

For flow, quality, operations, security and control, product outcomes, team experience and economics, answer:

1. What changed from baseline?
2. How large and consistent was the change?
3. What evidence supports it?
4. What competing explanation exists?
5. Did effort or risk move elsewhere?
6. What limitation affects confidence?
7. Would the result likely hold under the same conditions?

## Review failures and friction

Identify recurring context gaps, unreliable outputs, review burden, denied actions, incidents, support needs and workarounds. Distinguish a one-time onboarding problem from a structural condition that will recur.

## Identify decision constraints

Explicitly record any:

- material quality deterioration;
- security, safety or control deterioration;
- unresolved incident or evidence gap;
- unsustainable participant burden;
- dependence on exceptional individual effort;
- inability to reproduce or explain the result.

These constraints cannot be averaged away by faster delivery elsewhere.

## Stop here if

Evidence definitions changed without explanation, material negative evidence is missing, participants cannot report freely, or the comparison is too weak to support a decision. The correct response may be to gather more evidence under the same scope, not to expand.
