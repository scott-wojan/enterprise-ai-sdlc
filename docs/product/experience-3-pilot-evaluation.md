# Gate 5 Experience 3: Evaluate a Pilot and Present the Leadership Decision

## Status and scope

This is the third end-user content experience required by [Gate 5](https://github.com/scott-wojan/enterprise-ai-sdlc/issues/140).

It defines exact reader-facing content for evaluating a completed adoption pilot across the complete affected software-delivery path, selecting an evidence conclusion, restricting authority, creating remediation and presenting a leadership decision. It does not define visual design, storage, authentication, software architecture or production implementation. It does not change the current production site.

## User situation and representative scenario

The Account Services delivery team completed the bounded pilot prepared in Experience 2 after the earlier readiness hard stops were cleared through verified remediation and a new launch decision.

The representative pilot used AI to make bounded changes for authentication defect corrections in Service A. Implementation became faster. Review, correction, escaped defects, code churn, production-support effort, participant burden and total cost became worse.

The scenario is fictional and exists to test the product's decision logic. Its values are not external benchmarks or claims about AI-assisted development generally.

### Evaluated scope

- **Organization:** Example enterprise
- **Team:** Account Services delivery team
- **Service:** Service A
- **Work type:** Authentication defect corrections
- **Pilot posture:** Bounded Change
- **Pilot period:** 1 May through 15 June 2026
- **Production-support observation:** through 29 June 2026
- **Pilot cohort:** 24 eligible defect corrections
- **Baseline cohort:** 42 comparable defect corrections completed during the prior two quarters
- **Delivery boundary:** accepted defect through 14 days of production support
- **Evidence snapshot frozen:** 30 June 2026
- **Independent reviewer:** principal measurement analyst who did not perform or sponsor the pilot

### Representative results

- Implementation human effort decreased from 8.1 to 5.5 hours per eligible change.
- Total human effort across the complete delivery path increased from 23.5 to 27.7 hours per eligible change.
- Total elapsed time increased from 5.0 to 5.6 days per eligible change.
- Review effort increased from 3.6 to 5.8 hours per eligible change.
- Correction and rework effort increased from 2.2 to 4.1 hours per eligible change.
- Escaped authentication defects increased from 3 of 42 baseline changes to 4 of 24 pilot changes.
- Changed-line rework within 14 days increased from 6 percent to 13 percent.
- Security findings did not materially change, but security-review effort increased.
- Production-support effort increased from 4.7 to 6.5 hours per eligible change.
- Change failures increased from 1 of 42 baseline changes to 2 of 24 pilot changes.
- Nine of twelve respondents reported higher cognitive load, and four reported lower confidence in their ability to understand and support the resulting changes.
- Total cost increased from $3,400 to $4,130 per eligible change after implementation, review, correction, support, AI-specific and shared costs were included.
- The intended customer and service outcome showed no material improvement.

## Intended outcome

After completing this experience, the user should be able to:

- freeze the exact evidence reviewed by the decision;
- determine whether the baseline and pilot remain comparable;
- identify missing, limited, adverse and dissenting evidence;
- evaluate every value dimension separately;
- understand why faster implementation is not overall productivity or value;
- select the evidence conclusion supported by the complete record;
- choose an operating consequence that matches the evidence;
- obtain required specialized category decisions;
- restrict AI authority without stopping all lower-risk assistance;
- define remediation and the evidence required before reconsideration;
- produce a tangible-value assessment, decision memorandum and leadership briefing.

The representative result must be **negative net value demonstrated**. The organization must not make a productivity, savings or success claim, must not expand and must restrict the scope from Bounded Change to Assisted Work.

## Entry point and prerequisites

### Entry point

The user selected **Evaluate tangible value** from Experience 1 or entered from the completed pilot's current posture.

### Required prerequisites

The evaluation requires:

- the exact approved pilot scope and posture;
- the launch decision and approved evidence plan;
- the frozen baseline definition;
- completed pilot and downstream observation periods;
- linked evidence across the declared delivery boundary;
- total material cost;
- applicable guardrail evidence;
- adverse and dissenting evidence;
- independent review;
- category-owner participation.

If a material prerequisite is missing or unreliable, the product must select **value not demonstrated** and block claims and expansion. This representative scenario has enough verified evidence to reach a negative-value conclusion.

## Exact content order

The reader-facing experience appears in this order:

1. Decision being prepared.
2. Exact scope, current posture and proposed decision.
3. Freeze the evidence snapshot.
4. Check baseline comparability.
5. Check evidence completeness and limitations.
6. Review intended product or business outcome.
7. Review delivery flow and total human effort.
8. Review quality and maintainability.
9. Review security and control.
10. Review release and operations.
11. Review team experience and capability.
12. Review total economics.
13. Preserve adverse and dissenting evidence.
14. Select the evidence conclusion.
15. Choose the operating consequence.
16. Obtain specialized decisions.
17. State what may continue and what must stop.
18. Create remediation and reassessment.
19. Generate the tangible-value assessment.
20. Generate the decision memorandum.
21. Generate the leadership briefing.
22. Record the accountable decision and next review.

**Decision being prepared**

Should the Account Services delivery team stop, restrict, remediate, continue or become eligible to evaluate one bounded expansion of AI-assisted authentication defect corrections in Service A?

**Outputs produced**

- Tangible-value assessment
- Decision memorandum
- Leadership briefing
- Updated authority and current posture
- Remediation and reassessment plan

### Current posture and proposed decision

**Current posture before evaluation**

- Journey stage: Evaluate and decide
- Current AI participation: Bounded Change
- Work status: Pilot completed, decision pending
- Current gate decision: Approved pilot period ended
- Evidence conclusion: Decision pending
- Expansion authority: None

**Evaluation rule**

The result applies only to the completed Service A authentication-defect pilot. It does not automatically apply to another team, service, work type, repository, delivery activity, model or level of authority.

### Decision unit 1: Freeze the evidence snapshot

**Question**

Has the exact evidence reviewed by this decision been frozen, linked and preserved?

**Why it matters**

A decision cannot be reconstructed when reports continue changing, unfavorable records disappear or calculations are replaced without history. The evidence snapshot binds the conclusion to the facts reviewed at that time.

**Accountable owner**

Measurement owner

**Required participants**

Enterprise AI adoption lead, finance owner, evidence source owners, independent reviewer and affected category owners

**Guidance for answering**

Freeze source references, versions, periods, queries, calculations, exclusions, limitations and reviewer dispositions. Preserve later corrections as new versions rather than rewriting the reviewed snapshot.

**Structured response choices**

- Frozen and verified
- Frozen with material limitations
- Incomplete
- Material sources still changing
- Unknown

**Evidence expected**

- Snapshot identifier and date
- Exact scope and period
- Baseline and pilot cohort definitions
- Source versions and stable references
- Calculation version
- Evidence status and reviewer disposition
- Known limitations and conflicts
- Access classification and retention owner

**Scenario response**

Frozen and verified. The 30 June 2026 snapshot contains the baseline, pilot work, review, testing, security, release, production-support, participant and cost records used in this evaluation. Later changes require a new snapshot and decision version.

**Immediate interpretation**

The evidence reviewed by this decision can be reconstructed.

**What may continue**

Evidence completeness and comparability review.

**What must stop**

No source, exclusion, calculation or adverse record may be silently replaced in this decision version.

**Output affected**

Every generated report and accountable decision.

### Decision unit 2: Baseline comparability

**Question**

Are the baseline and pilot cohorts comparable enough to support the intended conclusion?

**Why it matters**

A difference may come from changed work complexity, staffing, release conditions or another delivery change rather than AI assistance. Comparability must be reviewed before interpreting results.

**Accountable owner**

Measurement owner

**Required participants**

Product owner, delivery lead, quality owner, service owner, finance owner and independent reviewer

**Guidance for answering**

Compare work type, eligibility rules, complexity, team, service, delivery boundary, observation period, policy, tooling and material outside changes. Record limitations rather than pretending the cohorts are identical.

**Structured response choices**

- Comparable for the stated conclusion
- Comparable with material limitations
- Comparable only for a narrower conclusion
- Not comparable
- Unknown

**Written question**

Why are the cohorts comparable, and what differences limit the conclusion?

**Insufficient response example**

> Both groups contain authentication defects from the same team, so the comparison is fair.

**Why it is insufficient**

- It does not address eligibility, complexity or delivery boundary.
- It does not identify outside changes.
- It does not state limitations.
- It cannot show which conclusions remain supportable.

**Useful response example**

> Both cohorts contain accepted authentication defect corrections in Service A completed by the same team under the same release and support process. Eligibility rules, complexity classification, start event, end event and 14-day support period are unchanged. The pilot cohort contains a slightly higher share of medium-complexity defects, so results are also reported within complexity bands. No material staffing, policy, release or observability change occurred during the pilot. The independent reviewer accepted comparison of total effort, elapsed time, quality, operations and cost, with a limitation that the pilot cohort of 24 changes produces wider uncertainty than the 42-change baseline.

**Why it is useful**

- It describes the matching conditions.
- It identifies a real difference and its treatment.
- It states what conclusions remain supportable.
- It records reviewer disposition and uncertainty.

**Minimum specificity required**

Address work type, cohort rules, complexity, team, service, boundary, observation period, outside changes, differences, treatment, supported conclusions, reviewer and limitations.

**Evidence expected**

Cohort definitions, complexity review, change log for outside factors, stratified calculation and independent reviewer disposition.

**Scenario response**

Comparable with the stated limitation. The representative organization uses the useful response above.

**Immediate interpretation**

The comparison can support this scoped evaluation. The sample-size and complexity limitations must appear in every report and prevent broader generalization.

**What may continue**

Dimension-level evaluation for the exact scope.

**What must stop**

The result must not be generalized to other teams, services or work types.

**Output affected**

Claim boundary and limitations in all evaluation reports.

### Decision unit 3: Evidence completeness

**Question**

Is verified evidence available for every material outcome dimension, guardrail and total-cost category?

**Why it matters**

A complete-looking report can still omit the downstream activity where effort or harm moved. Material missing evidence changes the conclusion to value not demonstrated.

**Accountable owner**

Enterprise AI adoption lead

**Required participants**

Measurement, product, delivery, quality, security, release, service, participant and finance owners, plus the independent reviewer

**Guidance for answering**

Review each evidence requirement against this exact decision. A linked source is not enough. Only evidence verified for the stated use satisfies a material requirement.

**Structured evidence review**

For each requirement show:

- Outcome dimension or guardrail
- Expected evidence
- Present, missing, stale, limited or rejected
- Exact scope and period
- Source and owner
- Verification status
- Limitation
- Claim or decision affected

**Written question when evidence is limited**

How does the limitation change the conclusion or claim boundary?

**Insufficient response example**

> The participant survey had a lower response rate, but the other metrics are complete.

**Why it is insufficient**

- It does not state the response rate or affected population.
- It does not explain selection effects.
- It does not state whether the team-experience conclusion remains supportable.
- It treats other favorable evidence as compensation.

**Useful response example**

> Twelve of fourteen eligible participants responded to the end-of-pilot pulse. The two nonrespondents were both reviewers, so review-burden evidence may understate deterioration. The participant evidence remains usable as a limitation because time records and reviewer observations independently show increased review effort. The report does not claim that every participant experienced the same effect and preserves the two missing responses as unknown.

**Why it is useful**

- It identifies the population and missingness.
- It explains the likely selection effect.
- It connects supporting evidence without erasing uncertainty.
- It narrows the claim appropriately.

**Minimum specificity required**

Name the missing or limited evidence, population, scope, likely effect, supporting or conflicting evidence, claim affected and reviewer disposition.

**Evidence expected**

Decision-specific evidence package with verified statuses, limitations, missingness and reviewer dispositions.

**Scenario response**

Material evidence is complete enough to make a scoped conclusion. Participant evidence has the limitation described above. No material baseline, downstream, cost, guardrail or independent-review requirement is missing.

**Immediate interpretation**

The organization must not use value not demonstrated merely to avoid acknowledging an unfavorable complete result. The available evidence supports a negative-value conclusion for this exact scope.

**What may continue**

Dimension-level evaluation.

**What must stop**

No limitation may be omitted from generated reports or used to discard adverse evidence selectively.

**Output affected**

Evidence conclusion and claim limits.

## Evaluate each outcome dimension separately

### Shared dimension review instructions

Each dimension remains separate. The product does not calculate an average productivity, readiness, value or return-on-investment score.

For each dimension, reviewers see:

- intended result and predeclared interpretation rule;
- baseline value and source;
- pilot value and source;
- practical difference;
- evidence status;
- limitations and confounders;
- adverse and dissenting evidence;
- provisional result;
- accountable reviewer disposition;
- implication for the final decision.

The available dimension results are:

- Improved
- No material change
- Deteriorated
- Unknown
- Not applicable with accountable justification

### Decision unit 4: Product or business outcome

**Question**

Did the pilot produce the intended customer, user, service or business result?

**Why it matters**

More output or faster implementation is not valuable if the work does not improve the outcome the organization intended to affect.

**Accountable owner**

Product owner

**Required participants**

Delivery lead, service owner, measurement owner and independent reviewer

**Scenario evidence**

Eligible authentication defects were completed, but the accepted-defect backlog, affected-user impact and service outcome did not materially improve relative to the comparable baseline period.

**Structured response**

- Result: No material change
- Evidence status: Verified
- Limitation: Pilot scope and duration were not designed to establish broader product impact

**Required rationale example that is insufficient**

> The pilot completed 24 defects, so it delivered customer value.

**Why it is insufficient**

- It equates completed work with an improved outcome.
- It does not compare the baseline.
- It does not identify customer or service effect.

**Required rationale example that is useful**

> The pilot completed its eligible authentication-defect work, but the accepted-defect backlog, affected-user incidents and authentication-service outcome showed no material improvement against the comparable baseline. Completion volume is recorded as activity, not product-value evidence.

**Why it is useful**

- It separates activity from outcome.
- It uses the approved comparison.
- It preserves the limited scope of the conclusion.

**Immediate interpretation**

The product or business outcome did not materially improve. Faster implementation cannot be justified as customer value from this evidence.

### Decision unit 5: Delivery flow and total human effort

**Question**

Did the complete delivery path become faster or require less total human effort?

**Why it matters**

Implementation savings can be transferred to review, correction, waiting, release or support. The complete path governs the result.

**Accountable owner**

Delivery lead

**Required participants**

Engineering, quality, release, service, measurement and participant representatives

**Scenario evidence**

- Implementation effort: 8.1 to 5.5 hours per eligible change, improved
- Review effort: 3.6 to 5.8 hours, deteriorated
- Correction and rework effort: 2.2 to 4.1 hours, deteriorated
- Production-support effort: 4.7 to 6.5 hours, deteriorated
- Total human effort: 23.5 to 27.7 hours, deteriorated
- Total elapsed time: 5.0 to 5.6 days, deteriorated

**Structured response**

- Result: Deteriorated
- Evidence status: Verified
- Limitation: Scoped to the eligible pilot cohort

**Required rationale example that is insufficient**

> Developer effort decreased by 32 percent, demonstrating a strong productivity improvement.

**Why it is insufficient**

- It reports only implementation.
- It omits increased review, rework and support.
- It ignores higher total effort and elapsed time.
- It uses the word productivity for a local activity result.

**Required rationale example that is useful**

> Implementation effort decreased by 2.6 hours per eligible change. Increased review, correction and production-support work more than consumed that saving. Total human effort increased by 4.2 hours, and total elapsed time increased by 0.6 days per eligible change. Delivery flow and total effort therefore deteriorated even though implementation became faster.

**Why it is useful**

- It preserves the local benefit.
- It includes transferred downstream effort.
- It reports the complete result.
- It supports the negative-value conclusion without hiding favorable evidence.

**Immediate interpretation**

Implementation became faster, but the complete delivery path became slower and required more human effort. No productivity claim is permitted.

### Decision unit 6: Quality and maintainability

**Question**

Did the pilot maintain or improve review quality, defect performance, rework and maintainability?

**Why it matters**

Faster initial output creates negative value when people must correct more work, defects escape or the resulting code becomes harder to maintain.

**Accountable owner**

Quality owner

**Required participants**

Engineering lead, reviewers, service owner, measurement owner and independent reviewer

**Scenario evidence**

- Review effort increased from 3.6 to 5.8 hours per eligible change.
- Correction and rework effort increased from 2.2 to 4.1 hours.
- Escaped authentication defects increased from 3 of 42 baseline changes to 4 of 24 pilot changes.
- Changed-line rework within 14 days increased from 6 percent to 13 percent.
- Reviewers recorded repeated issues with misunderstood service behavior and incomplete edge-case treatment.

**Structured response**

- Result: Deteriorated
- Evidence status: Verified
- Limitation: Small pilot cohort requires caution about exact rates but does not erase the direction and material burden

**Required rationale example that is insufficient**

> Quality was acceptable because every change passed tests and human review before release.

**Why it is insufficient**

- Passing before release does not erase increased review and correction effort.
- It omits escaped defects and churn.
- It treats control completion as proof of a favorable outcome.

**Required rationale example that is useful**

> Required tests and reviews operated, but they detected and corrected substantially more problems. Review and correction effort increased, escaped-defect incidence was higher and changed-line rework more than doubled. The small cohort limits the precision of exact rates, but the combined evidence shows a material deterioration in quality and maintainability for this scope.

**Why it is useful**

- It acknowledges that controls operated.
- It evaluates the burden and outcomes those controls revealed.
- It discloses the sample limitation.
- It states the scoped consequence.

**Immediate interpretation**

Quality and maintainability deteriorated. The fact that human review caught some problems does not convert the added burden into value.

### Decision unit 7: Security and control

**Question**

Did security exposure, control operation or control burden materially change?

**Why it matters**

Security and control results cannot be traded away for speed or cost. Failed controls, boundary violations or unacceptable exposure govern the decision independently.

**Accountable owner**

Security owner

**Required participants**

Platform owner, engineering lead, quality owner, measurement owner and independent reviewer

**Scenario evidence**

- No critical or material security finding occurred.
- No access or authority boundary was exceeded.
- Required security checks operated for every eligible change.
- Security-review effort increased by 24 percent because reviewers investigated more ambiguous authentication behavior.

**Structured response**

- Result: No material change in security outcome
- Control burden: Deteriorated and included in total effort and cost
- Evidence status: Verified
- Limitation: Applies only to the approved Service A scope and pilot tools

**Required rationale example that is insufficient**

> Security passed, so the pilot was safe and should continue.

**Why it is insufficient**

- A security result does not decide quality, operations, economics or continuation.
- It ignores increased review burden.
- It overgeneralizes beyond the assessed scope.

**Required rationale example that is useful**

> No material security finding or boundary violation occurred, and required controls operated for the exact pilot scope. Security-review effort increased by 24 percent and is included in total effort and cost. The security result is no material change, not evidence that the overall pilot succeeded or should continue.

**Why it is useful**

- It reports the favorable security outcome accurately.
- It preserves the increased control burden.
- It prevents one category from deciding the overall result.

**Immediate interpretation**

Security outcomes remained within the approved boundary, but security-review burden increased. This favorable guardrail result cannot offset deterioration elsewhere.

### Decision unit 8: Release and operations

**Question**

Did release reliability, production support, change failure or recovery materially change?

**Why it matters**

Work is not complete when code is produced or merged. Production effects and support burden are part of the delivery result.

**Accountable owner**

Service owner

**Required participants**

Release owner, engineering lead, support participants, measurement owner and independent reviewer

**Scenario evidence**

- Production-support effort increased from 4.7 to 6.5 hours per eligible change.
- Change failures increased from 1 of 42 baseline changes to 2 of 24 pilot changes.
- Both pilot change failures were recovered through the approved process.
- No severe service incident occurred.

**Structured response**

- Result: Deteriorated
- Evidence status: Verified
- Limitation: Small number of change failures requires caution about exact rates

**Required rationale example that is insufficient**

> Operations remained stable because there were no severe incidents and both failed changes were recovered.

**Why it is insufficient**

- It ignores higher support effort.
- It treats successful recovery as proof that failures did not matter.
- It omits the increased change-failure signal.

**Required rationale example that is useful**

> No severe incident occurred, and approved recovery worked for both pilot change failures. Production-support effort increased by 1.8 hours per eligible change, and the pilot had two failures across 24 changes compared with one across 42 baseline changes. The small counts limit the precision of the rate comparison, but the combined support and failure evidence shows operational deterioration for this scope.

**Why it is useful**

- It preserves successful recovery evidence.
- It includes support burden and failures.
- It discloses uncertainty.
- It states the scoped result.

**Immediate interpretation**

Release and operational outcomes deteriorated. Recovery success reduced impact but did not erase the added failures and support work.

### Decision unit 9: Team experience and capability

**Question**

Could participants review, understand, support and sustain the pilot work without unacceptable burden or loss of confidence?

**Why it matters**

An approach that depends on exhausted reviewers, hidden expert intervention or declining confidence is not a sustainable delivery improvement.

**Accountable owner**

Delivery leader

**Required participants**

Participant representative, workforce or people partner, quality owner, service owner and independent reviewer

**Scenario evidence**

- Twelve of fourteen eligible participants completed the end-of-pilot pulse.
- Nine respondents reported higher cognitive load.
- Four reported lower confidence in understanding and supporting AI-assisted changes.
- Reviewers described repeated context reconstruction and uncertainty about whether generated changes respected service behavior.
- Two nonrespondents were reviewers, so the observed burden may be understated.

**Structured response**

- Result: Deteriorated
- Evidence status: Verified with limitation
- Limitation: Two missing reviewer responses remain unknown

**Required rationale example that is insufficient**

> Most team members completed the pilot, and the team gained useful AI experience.

**Why it is insufficient**

- Completion does not establish sustainable experience.
- It omits cognitive load and lower confidence.
- It does not account for nonresponse.
- Learning cannot cancel participant harm or burden.

**Required rationale example that is useful**

> Nine of twelve respondents reported higher cognitive load, and four reported lower confidence in understanding and supporting the resulting changes. Reviewer observations and time records support the reported burden. Two eligible reviewers did not respond, so the result may understate rather than overstate the problem. The team learned from the pilot, but team sustainability deteriorated for this operating approach.

**Why it is useful**

- It preserves the learning benefit.
- It reports attributable adverse evidence.
- It explains missingness.
- It distinguishes learning from sustainable operation.

**Immediate interpretation**

Team experience and capability deteriorated. Continued Bounded Change would expose reviewers and support participants to a burden the current evidence does not justify.

### Decision unit 10: Total economics

**Question**

Did the complete pilot produce more valuable results with lower total effort or cost?

**Why it matters**

An economic result must include the full delivery path and AI-specific costs. Labor savings in implementation are not savings when review, rework, support and platform costs create a larger increase.

**Accountable owner**

Finance owner

**Required participants**

Measurement owner, delivery lead, platform owner, quality owner, service owner and independent reviewer

**Scenario evidence**

- Total baseline cost: $3,400 per eligible change
- Total pilot cost: $4,130 per eligible change
- Difference: $730 more per eligible change
- Implementation labor cost decreased by $420 per eligible change.
- Increased review, correction and support cost exceeded the implementation saving.
- AI-specific model, token, tool and allocated platform cost added $220 per eligible change.
- Training, governance and control cost are included using the approved pilot allocation.
- No monetized product benefit was demonstrated.

**Structured response**

- Result: Deteriorated
- Evidence status: Verified
- Finance disposition: Economic benefit not demonstrated; total cost increased
- Limitation: Applies to this pilot scale and allocation method

**Required rationale example that is insufficient**

> The pilot saved $420 of developer time per change, and platform costs should decline at scale.

**Why it is insufficient**

- It reports only implementation labor.
- It ignores the verified $730 total-cost increase.
- It assumes untested future scale savings.
- It omits the absence of product benefit.

**Required rationale example that is useful**

> Implementation labor decreased by $420 per eligible change. Increased review, correction and production-support cost, together with $220 of AI-specific and allocated platform cost, produced a verified total increase of $730 per eligible change. No material product benefit offset that increase. The result is an economic deterioration for this exact scope. Possible future scale effects are not evidence in this decision.

**Why it is useful**

- It preserves the implementation saving.
- It includes transferred and AI-specific costs.
- It connects cost to product outcome.
- It rejects unsupported future assumptions.

**Immediate interpretation**

The complete pilot became more expensive. No savings, return-on-investment or productivity claim is permitted.

## Preserve adverse and dissenting evidence

### Decision unit 11: Adverse and dissenting evidence

**Question**

Does the decision package preserve evidence for and against continuation, including participant disagreement?

**Why it matters**

Leadership cannot make a defensible decision from a report that celebrates local speed and hides downstream cost, defects, burden or dissent.

**Accountable owner**

Enterprise AI adoption lead

**Required participants**

All category owners, participant representative and independent reviewer

**Structured review**

Confirm that the package includes:

- Faster implementation
- Increased total effort and elapsed time
- Increased review and correction
- Quality and churn deterioration
- Security result and increased control burden
- Operational deterioration
- Participant burden and missing responses
- Higher total cost
- No material product-outcome improvement
- Limitations and confounders
- Category-owner positions
- Independent reviewer challenge
- Any dissenting recommendation

**Written question**

What material disagreement remains, and what interim restriction governs it?

**Insufficient response example**

> Some team members remain concerned, but leadership supports continued innovation.

**Why it is insufficient**

- It does not identify the disputed condition.
- It does not preserve the evidence or accountable position.
- It substitutes leadership enthusiasm for disposition.
- It states no governing restriction.

**Useful response example**

> The pilot lead recommends a limited remediation trial because implementation effort improved. The quality and service owners reject continued Bounded Change because review, correction, escaped defects and support effort deteriorated. The participant representative supports continued Assisted Work but not AI state-changing authority. The more restrictive position governs: Bounded Change stops, Assisted Work may continue within its prior boundary and leadership must decide whether to fund remediation or stop the use case.

**Why it is useful**

- It records each material position.
- It connects positions to evidence and authority.
- It applies the current restriction.
- It presents leadership with a real decision.

**Minimum specificity required**

Name each position, evidence, authority category, interim restriction, escalation owner and decision required.

**Scenario response**

The representative package preserves the disagreement described in the useful response.

**Immediate interpretation**

The pilot lead's preference for remediation does not authorize continued Bounded Change. The quality, service and participant positions require restriction while leadership decides whether to fund corrective work.

## Select the evidence conclusion

### Decision unit 12: Evidence conclusion

**Question**

What conclusion does the complete evidence support for this exact scope?

**Why it matters**

The conclusion states what the organization can defensibly claim. It is separate from the operating decision about what leadership chooses to fund or permit next.

**Accountable owner**

Enterprise AI adoption lead

**Required participants**

Independent reviewer and every affected category owner

**Response choices**

- Value not evaluated
- Value not demonstrated
- Negative net value demonstrated
- No material net improvement demonstrated
- Positive net value demonstrated
- Mixed result

**Guidance for selection**

- Select **value not demonstrated** when material evidence is missing or unreliable.
- Select **negative net value demonstrated** when complete evidence shows materially worse total value, effort, cost, quality, security, operations, maintainability, team sustainability or business outcome.
- Do not select **mixed result** merely because one local activity improved. A local benefit within an overall materially worse system does not require a mixed conclusion.

**Scenario selection**

Negative net value demonstrated

**Rationale**

Implementation effort improved, but the complete evidence shows higher total human effort, longer elapsed time, worse review and correction burden, more escaped defects, more code churn, greater production-support effort, worse participant experience and higher total cost without a material product-outcome improvement. Security outcomes remained acceptable but required more review effort. The overall system became materially worse for this exact scope.

**Claim boundary**

- No productivity claim
- No savings claim
- No positive-value claim
- No success claim
- No expansion or standardization claim
- Implementation-effort reduction may be reported only as a local observation beside the complete negative result

**Immediate interpretation**

The completed pilot demonstrated negative net value. Continued Bounded Change and any expansion are not justified.

## Choose the operating consequence

### Decision unit 13: Recommended operating decision

**Question**

What should happen to AI participation for this exact scope now?

**Why it matters**

The evidence conclusion does not change authority automatically. Accountable people must choose an operating consequence that respects category decisions and hard stops.

**Accountable owner**

Enterprise AI adoption lead, with executive sponsor ownership of investment and category owners deciding within their authority

**Required participants**

Quality, security, release, service, finance, product, delivery, participant and independent-review owners

**Response choices**

- Stop AI use for the exact scope
- Pause pending investigation
- Remediate under a lower-authority posture
- Restrict scope or authority
- Continue the exact current scope
- Evaluate one bounded expansion

Options unsupported by the current evidence remain unavailable.

Production-support effort increased by 1.8 hours per change, and the pilot had two change failures across 24 changes compared with one across 42 baseline changes. Recovery operated as designed.

Nine of twelve respondents reported higher cognitive load, four reported lower confidence and two missing reviewer responses remain unknown.

**Economics: Deteriorated**

Total cost increased from $3,400 to $4,130 per eligible change. The $420 implementation-labor saving was more than consumed by downstream and AI-specific cost. No material product benefit offset the increase.
