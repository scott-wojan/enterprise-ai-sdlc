# Gate 5 Experience 2: Determine Whether Responsible Pilot Work Can Begin

## Status and scope

This is the second end-user content experience required by [Gate 5](https://github.com/scott-wojan/enterprise-ai-sdlc/issues/140).

It defines exact reader-facing content for a readiness decision that identifies hard stops, preserves honest unknowns, creates owned remediation and produces team and leadership outputs. It does not define visual design, storage, authentication, software architecture or production implementation. It does not change the current production site.

## User situation and representative scenario

An enterprise AI adoption lead is preparing a controlled pilot of AI-assisted software delivery.

The representative organization has:

- an accountable executive sponsor;
- a named enterprise AI adoption lead;
- a defined team, service and work type;
- a proposed Bounded Change posture;
- a trustworthy baseline for comparable work;
- named independent measurement review;
- a documented stop procedure that has not been demonstrated;
- no reliable way to attribute production-support effort to the pilot work;
- an incomplete total-cost view because support effort and several shared costs are unknown.

The proposed scope is:

- **Organization:** Example enterprise
- **Team:** Account Services delivery team
- **Service:** Service A
- **Work type:** Authentication defect corrections
- **Software-delivery activities:** analysis, implementation, review, testing, security checking, release and 14 days of production support
- **Proposed AI participation:** Bounded Change in a nonproduction branch, followed by deterministic verification and accountable human review
- **Proposed evaluation period:** six weeks
- **Proposed outcome:** reduce total elapsed time and human effort from accepted defect through 14 days of production support without unacceptable deterioration in quality, security, operations, maintainability, team experience or total cost

## Intended outcome

After completing this experience, the user should be able to:

- explain the readiness decision being made;
- distinguish executive sponsorship from specialized decision authority;
- name the adoption lead and demonstrate stop authority;
- confirm that participants can report adverse evidence safely;
- define an outcome across the complete affected delivery path;
- establish comparable baseline evidence;
- identify missing downstream evidence and total costs;
- understand each immediate consequence;
- see what may continue and what must stop;
- assign specific remediation and verification;
- present leadership with a direct decision request.

The representative result must not authorize an outcome-evaluated pilot, a value claim or expansion.

## Entry point and prerequisites

### Entry point

The user selected **Assess readiness** from Experience 1 and reviewed the purpose, likely participants, outputs and evidence expectations before entering the adoption workspace.

### Information required to begin

The user needs enough information to define:

- the organization, team, service, work type and software-delivery activities being assessed;
- the proposed type of AI participation;
- the executive sponsor and adoption lead;
- known category decision owners;
- the intended outcome;
- available baseline, downstream and cost evidence.

Missing information is allowed. It remains visible and may restrict or block the proposed action.

### Information protection notice

Do not paste passwords, access tokens, private keys, regulated records, customer data or unnecessary sensitive content into the workspace. Link to an approved protected source when evidence should remain in its system of record.

## Complete end-user copy

Everything in this section is reader-facing copy unless marked as helper text.

### Workspace entry

**Heading**

Determine whether responsible pilot work can begin

**Opening copy**

This assessment determines whether the exact proposed work has the ownership, stop authority, engineering conditions, evidence and cost visibility needed for real AI-assisted delivery.

It is not a survey and it does not produce a readiness percentage. A favorable answer cannot cancel a material failure. If a condition blocks the work, you will see the consequence immediately, along with what may continue, what must stop and what must be demonstrated before reassessment.

**Decision being prepared**

May the Account Services delivery team begin a six-week, outcome-evaluated pilot of AI-assisted authentication defect corrections in Service A under the proposed Bounded Change posture?

**Output produced**

A readiness decision, evidence-gap and remediation record, and leadership decision request.

### Exact assessed scope

**Heading**

Confirm the exact scope

**Why it matters**

Readiness and approval apply only to the work, people, systems, data, tools, environments and authority that were assessed. Approval for one scope does not authorize another.

**Accountable owner**

Enterprise AI adoption lead

**Required participants**

Pilot lead, product owner, engineering lead, platform owner, quality owner, security owner, release owner, service owner, measurement owner, finance owner and participant representative

**Structured response**

Confirm or change each scoped item:

- Organization
- Team
- Product or service
- Repository or component
- Work type
- Software-delivery activities
- Included data and context
- AI identity or approved identity class
- Allowed tools
- Proposed actions
- Environment
- Downstream effects included
- Proposed adoption posture
- Start, end and review dates

**Helper text**

Select exact entities where possible. Do not enter “engineering,” “all repositories” or “all delivery work” unless the evidence and authority genuinely cover that scope.

**Scenario response**

The representative scope shown above is confirmed. The Bounded Change proposal is limited to authentication defect corrections in Service A, one delivery team, one nonproduction branch pattern and the complete path through 14 days of production support.

**Immediate interpretation**

The scope is specific enough to assess. This does not mean it is ready or approved.

**What may continue**

Readiness assessment and evidence preparation for this exact scope.

**What must stop**

Any attempt to treat this assessment as permission for a different team, service, repository, work type, environment or level of AI authority.

**Output affected**

Readiness decision and all later reports.

### How to read the current posture

**Heading**

The assessment keeps different facts separate

**Copy**

The workspace shows:

- **Current AI participation:** what type of AI use is currently allowed for this scope;
- **Current work status:** whether work is being prepared, performed, paused, stopped or completed;
- **Current decision:** what accountable people have authorized now;
- **Evidence conclusion:** what the evidence does and does not support.

Completing questions does not change any of these facts by itself.

**Initial posture**

- Current AI participation: Foundation
- Work status: Preparing evidence
- Current decision: No pilot launch decision yet
- Evidence conclusion: Value not evaluated
- Proposed change: Authorize Bounded Change for the exact pilot scope

### Who must participate

**Heading**

One sponsor cannot make every required decision

**Copy**

The executive sponsor owns enterprise intent, investment and whether leadership will continue funding the work. The sponsor does not automatically own security, legal, financial, release, service or other specialized decisions.

Each affected category needs an authorized person who can approve, restrict or reject the proposal within that person's authority. The enterprise AI adoption lead combines those decisions into the overall adoption recommendation but cannot override them.

The delivery team must be able to report rework, friction, uncertainty, failure and unexpected cost without pressure to manufacture a success story.

### Decision unit 1: Accountable executive sponsorship

**Question**

Is one identifiable executive sponsor accountable for the adoption intent and investment decision for this exact scope?

**Why it matters**

The organization needs a person with authority to decide why the work is being considered, fund or stop it and require the right decision owners to participate. A committee name or general leadership support is not enough.

**Accountable owner**

Executive sponsor

**Required participants**

Enterprise AI adoption lead and finance owner

**Guidance for answering**

Choose **Demonstrated** only when one identifiable person has accepted accountability for the exact scope, investment boundary, adverse evidence and stop decision.

**Structured response choices**

- Demonstrated
- Named but acceptance not recorded
- Not yet established
- Unknown

**Named-person fields**

- Person
- Role
- Authority source
- Scope of authority
- Effective period
- Acceptance date

**Evidence expected**

A recorded adoption-intent or investment decision that names the person, exact scope, authority, effective period and acceptance of adverse evidence and stop recommendations.

**Scenario response**

Demonstrated. The Chief Technology Officer is the executive sponsor for the six-week Service A pilot assessment and any related investment decision. The signed adoption-intent decision states that unfavorable evidence may result in restriction or termination.

**Immediate interpretation**

Accountable executive sponsorship is demonstrated for this exact scope.

**What may continue**

The readiness assessment.

**What must stop**

The sponsor's approval cannot be treated as approval for specialized security, release, service or financial decisions.

**Evidence status**

Verified for this assessment period.

**Output affected**

Adoption intent and ownership section of the readiness report.

### Decision unit 2: Specialized category decision authority

**Question**

Are authorized owners named for every specialized decision affected by the proposed work?

**Why it matters**

The proposed work can affect security, quality, release, production support and cost. Leadership interest cannot replace the people who are accountable for those decisions.

**Accountable owner**

Enterprise AI adoption lead

**Required participants**

Executive sponsor and every affected category owner

**Guidance for answering**

Select every category affected by the exact scope. Name one person who has authority for each category and record whether that person has accepted the role.

**Structured response**

For each applicable category, capture:

- Category
- Named person
- Decision owned
- Authority source
- Accepted, pending or declined
- Effective period

Categories include:

- Product outcome
- Delivery
- Architecture and platform
- Quality and verification
- Security, privacy, legal and compliance
- Release and change
- Service and operations
- Measurement
- Finance
- Workforce or participant conditions

**Evidence expected**

Current responsibility or delegation records and recorded acceptance for the assessed decision.

**Scenario response**

All applicable category owners are named and have accepted their decision responsibilities. The service owner has accepted responsibility for production-support evidence but has not yet established the required attribution method.

**Immediate interpretation**

Specialized decision authority is present. A missing evidence method remains a separate readiness problem and is evaluated later.

**What may continue**

The readiness assessment and evidence preparation.

**What must stop**

No category decision may be inferred from the executive sponsor's general support.

**Output affected**

Decision-owner map and required category decisions in the leadership briefing.

### Decision unit 3: Enterprise AI adoption lead

**Question**

Is one identifiable person accountable for coordinating this adoption scope and enforcing its current decision?

**Why it matters**

Someone must keep the exact scope, current permission, evidence gaps, restrictions, remediation and next decision visible across organizational boundaries.

**Accountable owner**

Executive sponsor

**Required participants**

Named enterprise AI adoption lead and affected category owners

**Guidance for answering**

Choose **Demonstrated** only when the person has accepted responsibility and has enough organizational authority to coordinate the work, escalate unresolved category decisions and enforce a pause or stop.

**Structured response choices**

- Demonstrated
- Named but authority or acceptance is incomplete
- Not yet established
- Unknown

**Named-person fields**

- Person
- Role
- Responsibilities
- Authority source
- Escalation route
- Effective period
- Acceptance date

**Evidence expected**

A recorded assignment accepted by the named person and recognized by the sponsor and affected owners.

**Scenario response**

Demonstrated. The Director of Engineering Enablement is the enterprise AI adoption lead for this scope and has accepted responsibility for readiness coordination, current posture, escalation and stop enforcement.

**Immediate interpretation**

Adoption coordination is owned. The separate question of whether the stop path actually works remains open.

**What may continue**

The readiness assessment.

**What must stop**

The named role cannot be treated as proof that technical stop and revocation mechanisms operate.

**Output affected**

Readiness ownership and remediation accountability.

### Decision unit 4: Demonstrated human stop authority

**Question**

Can an authorized human stop the proposed work and enforce the stop without relying on the AI system to cooperate?

**Why it matters**

A written instruction to stop is not enough. If work leaves its approved scope, required checks fail or harm begins, an authorized person must be able to halt activity, revoke access and prevent further effects.

**Accountable owner**

Enterprise AI adoption lead

**Required participants**

Pilot lead, platform owner, security owner, repository owner, incident owner and service owner

**Guidance for answering**

Choose **Demonstrated** only after the exact pilot identity, tools, repository access and execution path have been exercised. The evidence must show who initiated the stop, what was disabled, how quickly it took effect, how the stopped state was verified and how evidence was preserved.

**Structured response choices**

- Demonstrated for the exact scope
- Documented but not demonstrated
- Demonstration failed
- Not yet established
- Unknown

**Written question**

Describe the human and technical stop path for this exact scope.

**Example that is not sufficient**

> The pilot lead can tell the team to stop, and administrators can disable the tool if needed.

**Why this is not sufficient**

- It does not identify the person authorized to declare the stop.
- It does not identify the AI identity, access, tool or action that will be disabled.
- It does not explain how the organization prevents further activity.
- It does not show that the path was tested.
- It does not explain evidence preservation or verification.

**Example that is useful**

> For the Service A pilot, the pilot lead, enterprise AI adoption lead, platform on-call lead or security incident lead may declare a stop. The platform on-call lead disables the named pilot identity, revokes its repository token and blocks the pilot tool at the approved access gateway. The repository owner verifies that no new branch, commit or pull request can be created. The security owner preserves gateway and repository logs. A dated exercise on 12 August used the actual pilot identity and completed revocation in four minutes. The pilot cannot restart until the platform and security owners verify the restored boundary and the enterprise AI adoption lead records a new decision.

**Why this is useful**

- It names authorized people.
- It identifies the exact identity, access and actions stopped.
- It describes enforcement that does not depend on AI cooperation.
- It includes operational proof.
- It names verification, evidence preservation and restart authority.

**Minimum specificity required**

Name the declarers, exact identity and access affected, enforcement actions, verification, evidence preserved, latest exercise date, observed result and restart decision owner.

**Evidence expected**

- Approved stop procedure for the exact scope
- Dated exercise record
- Access revocation and denial logs
- Repository or tool evidence showing further action was prevented
- Named verifier disposition
- Restart decision requirement

**Scenario response**

Documented but not demonstrated. The runbook names the pilot lead and platform owner, but the organization has not exercised access revocation with the proposed pilot identity. No evidence shows that new repository actions are prevented after a stop is declared.

**Immediate interpretation**

**Hard stop: No demonstrated human stop path**

The organization cannot show that an authorized person can halt the proposed work and enforce the halt. Real enterprise AI-assisted work for this scope must not begin.

**What may continue**

- Readiness assessment
- Non-AI planning and evidence collection
- Configuration of the stop path
- A nonproduction stop and revocation exercise using the proposed pilot identity
- Remediation review

**What must stop**

- Outcome-evaluated pilot launch
- Any real enterprise AI-assisted work in this scope
- Any representation that the proposed Bounded Change posture is ready

**Required remediation**

Demonstrate the complete stop, revocation, denial, evidence-preservation and restart path for the exact pilot identity and repository boundary.

**Evidence required for reassessment**

A successful dated exercise, source logs, independent verification by the platform and security owners and a new readiness decision.

**Output affected**

Current posture, hard-stop record, readiness decision and leadership briefing.

### Decision unit 5: Safe reporting of failure and adverse evidence

**Question**

Can participants report failure, rework, unsafe conditions and unexpected cost without pressure to protect the pilot's success story?

**Why it matters**

The evidence becomes unreliable when participants believe unfavorable information will be ignored, punished or used against them. A pilot designed to succeed cannot support an honest continue or stop decision.

**Accountable owner**

Executive sponsor

**Required participants**

Enterprise AI adoption lead, workforce or people partner, pilot lead and participant representative

**Guidance for answering**

Choose **Demonstrated** only when participants have a known reporting route, understand that adverse evidence is expected and can escalate outside the pilot lead when necessary.

**Structured response choices**

- Demonstrated
- Communicated but not confirmed
- Not yet established
- Participants report pressure or retaliation risk
- Unknown

**Written question**

How can participants report adverse evidence or request a stop?

**Example that is not sufficient**

> Team members can raise concerns in the weekly pilot meeting.

**Why this is not sufficient**

- It provides no route outside the pilot lead.
- It does not explain confidentiality or retaliation protection.
- It does not state who can act on a stop request.
- It gives no evidence that participants understand the route.

**Example that is useful**

> Participants may report issues to the pilot lead, the enterprise AI adoption lead or the workforce partner through the existing confidential reporting channel. Any participant may request an immediate pause when continued work could increase harm. The sponsor's pilot notice states that defects, rework, control failure, excess review burden and negative cost evidence are expected inputs and will not be treated as participant failure. All twelve participants acknowledged the route during onboarding, and the participant representative confirmed understanding through a private follow-up.

**Why this is useful**

- It provides more than one route.
- It explains when a pause may be requested.
- It protects unfavorable evidence.
- It includes evidence that participants understand the process.

**Minimum specificity required**

Name the reporting routes, escalation recipient, pause-request path, participant protection, communication method and evidence of understanding.

**Evidence expected**

Participant notice, onboarding acknowledgment, escalation route and attributable or appropriately protected confirmation from participants.

**Scenario response**

Demonstrated. The organization has communicated and confirmed the reporting, escalation and pause-request routes described in the useful example.

**Immediate interpretation**

Safe reporting is demonstrated for the assessed participants. This favorable condition does not cancel the open stop-path hard stop.

**What may continue**

Readiness preparation and participant engagement.

**What must stop**

Any attempt to describe silence or lack of complaints as evidence that the pilot is safe or successful.

**Output affected**

Participant readiness and evidence-reliability sections.

### Decision unit 6: Intended outcome and complete delivery boundary

**Question**

What outcome should this pilot improve, and where does the affected delivery path begin and end?

**Why it matters**

The organization cannot judge success from AI activity, code output or implementation time alone. The intended outcome must connect to valuable work and include every upstream and downstream activity that could transfer effort, cost or harm.

**Accountable owner**

Product owner for the intended outcome

**Required participants**

Delivery lead, engineering lead, quality owner, security owner, release owner, service owner, measurement owner, finance owner and participant representative

**Guidance for answering**

Define one work type and the complete comparison path. Name the intended result and the conditions that would make a local improvement unacceptable.

**Written question**

What outcome should improve, for which work, across what start and end boundary?

**Example that is not sufficient**

> Improve developer productivity and deliver authentication fixes faster.

**Why this is not sufficient**

- It does not define the comparable work.
- It does not define the start and end of the delivery path.
- It treats implementation speed as the result.
- It omits review, testing, security, release and production support.
- It does not protect quality, maintainability, team experience or total cost.

**Example that is useful**

> For accepted authentication defect corrections in Service A, determine whether AI assistance reduces total elapsed time and human effort from defect acceptance through 14 days of production support. Include analysis, implementation, review, security checking, testing, release, correction, rework and support. Do not treat the pilot as successful if defects, escaped defects, security findings, code churn, change failure, operational work, participant burden or total delivery cost becomes materially worse.

**Why this is useful**

- It identifies the exact work and service.
- It declares the comparison boundary.
- It includes upstream and downstream work.
- It names material constraints.
- It can support a continue, restrict, remediate or stop decision.

**Minimum specificity required**

Name the work type, service or system, comparison start, comparison end, affected activities, intended result and conditions that prevent a favorable conclusion.

**Structured supporting fields**

- Work type
- Eligible cohort
- Start event
- End event
- Included SDLC activities
- Intended result
- Guardrail categories
- Known exclusions and why

**Evidence expected**

Approved outcome statement, workflow definition, included activity owners and measurement review.

**Scenario response**

The useful response above is accepted as the representative outcome and delivery boundary.

**Immediate interpretation**

The proposed outcome is specific and includes the complete affected delivery path. Whether the organization can observe every included activity is evaluated separately.

**What may continue**

Baseline and evidence planning.

**What must stop**

Any attempt to redefine success as implementation speed, story count, generated code or tool usage.

**Output affected**

Pilot outcome, evidence plan and later tangible-value claim boundary.

### Decision unit 7: Comparable work and meaningful baseline

**Question**

Is there a trustworthy comparison for the exact outcome the pilot is intended to improve?

**Why it matters**

Without comparable historical or concurrent work, the organization cannot determine whether an observed change came from AI assistance, normal variation or another change in the delivery system.

**Accountable owner**

Measurement owner

**Required participants**

Product owner, delivery lead, quality owner, service owner, finance owner and independent reviewer

**Guidance for answering**

Choose **Verified** only when the baseline covers comparable work across the declared delivery boundary and its sources, period, calculation, exclusions and limitations can be reproduced.

**Structured response choices**

- Verified for the proposed outcome
- Available with material limitations
- Proposed but not yet assembled
- Unavailable
- Unknown

**Written question**

Describe the baseline cohort and why it is comparable.

**Example that is not sufficient**

> We will compare the pilot with our normal delivery metrics from last quarter.

**Why this is not sufficient**

- It does not identify the work type or population.
- It does not define which delivery activities are included.
- It does not name sources or exclusions.
- It does not explain material differences.
- It cannot be reproduced.

**Example that is useful**

> The baseline contains 42 accepted authentication defect corrections completed in Service A during the prior two quarters by the same delivery team. Each item has timestamps and human-effort records from acceptance through 14 days of production support. The cohort excludes two emergency security incidents and three changes bundled with unrelated feature work. Review, test, release, defect, churn and support records are linked by work-item and change identifier. The measurement owner reproduced the cohort on 8 August. The independent reviewer accepted the baseline with the limitation that seasonal on-call staffing may affect support effort.

**Why this is useful**

- It defines the population and period.
- It matches the intended work and delivery boundary.
- It identifies sources and exclusions.
- It discloses a limitation.
- It records independent review.

**Minimum specificity required**

Name the eligible work, population, period, source records, included activities, exclusions, material differences, calculation owner, independent reviewer and limitations.

**Evidence expected**

Reproducible cohort definition, source references, linked delivery records, calculation method, exclusions, limitations and reviewer disposition.

**Scenario response**

Verified for the proposed outcome. The representative organization has the baseline described in the useful example.

**Immediate interpretation**

A meaningful baseline exists for the declared outcome. This does not cure missing production-support evidence during the pilot or incomplete pilot cost.

**What may continue**

Evidence planning and readiness remediation.

**What must stop**

The baseline must not be reused for another team, service, work type or delivery boundary without a new applicability decision.

**Output affected**

Baseline section of the readiness report and later tangible-value assessment.

### Decision unit 8: Downstream evidence coverage

**Question**

Can the organization observe material effects across every affected software-delivery activity?

**Why it matters**

AI may save effort in one activity while transferring greater work or harm to review, testing, security, release, operations or support. Missing downstream evidence prevents the organization from proving tangible value.

**Accountable owner**

Measurement owner

**Required participants**

Delivery, quality, security, release, service, support, finance and participant representatives

**Guidance for answering**

For each included activity, identify the evidence source, owner, linkage method, observation period and whether the source can distinguish pilot work from other work.

**Structured response for each activity**

- Activity
- Evidence source
- Source owner
- Work-item linkage
- Observation period
- Actual, estimated or unavailable
- Review status
- Known limitations

Activities for this scope:

- Analysis
- Implementation
- Review and approval
- Testing and quality engineering
- Security checking and remediation
- Release and change
- Production support and operations
- Defect correction and rework
- Maintainability and code churn
- Participant effort and experience

**Written question when evidence is limited or unavailable**

What is missing, which decision does it prevent and how will the organization establish it?

**Example that is not sufficient**

> Operations data is difficult to connect, so we will use implementation and testing results for the pilot.

**Why this is not sufficient**

- It silently removes production support from the approved delivery boundary.
- It does not identify the missing evidence or owner.
- It would allow a local speed result to hide transferred support work.
- It does not state the claim and decisions that are blocked.

**Example that is useful**

> Production-support effort for Service A is recorded by weekly cost center, not by work item or change. The service owner cannot currently distinguish effort caused by pilot changes from other support work during the 14-day observation period. This prevents a complete downstream and tangible-value conclusion. The service owner will add the change identifier to support records for eligible pilot work and the measurement owner will reconcile work items, releases and support activity before launch. Until the method is tested against three historical changes, the outcome-evaluated pilot and every value or expansion claim remain blocked.

**Why this is useful**

- It names the exact missing downstream evidence.
- It explains why the current source is not usable.
- It identifies the decisions affected.
- It assigns corrective action and proof.
- It preserves the approved delivery boundary.

**Minimum specificity required**

Name the missing activity, source limitation, affected scope, claim or decision blocked, owner, corrective action, test and review requirement.

**Evidence expected**

Source definition, attribution method, owner acceptance, historical test, linked sample records and independent measurement verification.

**Scenario response**

Production-support evidence is unavailable at the required work-item level. All other listed downstream activities have identified sources and owners.

**Immediate interpretation**

**Hard stop for a tangible-value decision: Material downstream effects cannot be observed**

The organization cannot determine whether AI-assisted changes increase or decrease production-support effort. An outcome-evaluated pilot cannot launch under the approved evidence plan, and no value, productivity, savings, expansion or standardization claim may be made from this scope.

**What may continue**

- Readiness remediation
- Design and testing of the support-attribution method
- Historical evidence reconciliation
- Public guidance and non-AI planning
- After the human stop path is demonstrated, a separately approved and isolated learning-only exercise that makes no value claim

**What must stop**

- Outcome-evaluated pilot launch
- Tangible-value conclusion
- Productivity or savings claim
- Continuation or expansion decision based on claimed improvement

**Required remediation**

Establish and test a reliable method that links production-support effort to eligible pilot work across the declared observation period.

**Evidence required for reassessment**

Three historical linkage tests, source records, reconciliation results, service-owner acceptance and independent measurement verification.

**Output affected**

Evidence-gap register, readiness decision, claim limits and leadership briefing.

### Decision unit 9: Total material cost

**Question**

Can the organization establish the total material cost of the proposed pilot and the work it affects?

**Why it matters**

Time that appears to be saved can be outweighed by model, tool, integration, training, review, rework, support, governance or incident cost. An economic or productivity claim is not defensible when material cost is missing.

**Accountable owner**

Finance owner

**Required participants**

Executive sponsor, enterprise AI adoption lead, platform owner, delivery lead, measurement owner, service owner and independent reviewer

**Guidance for answering**

Record actual or estimated cost for every applicable category. Identify the source, allocation method, period, owner, included and excluded costs and uncertainty. A planned cost source is not evidence that the cost is available.

**Structured cost categories**

- Model and token usage
- Tool licenses
- Infrastructure
- Integration and technical enablement
- Training and onboarding
- Human review and approval
- Testing and control operation
- Correction and rework
- Release and change
- Production support and operations
- Governance and reporting
- Incidents and recovery
- Opportunity cost
- Shared cost allocation

For each category capture:

- Amount or range
- Currency
- Period
- Fixed or variable
- Actual, estimated or unavailable
- Source
- Allocation method
- Owner
- Included and excluded cost
- Limitation

**Written question when cost is estimated or unavailable**

What cost is missing or uncertain, how could it change the decision and how will it be established?

**Example that is not sufficient**

> The pilot will use existing licenses, so the main cost is team time. We expect the productivity gain to cover it.

**Why this is not sufficient**

- Existing licenses still have a cost and allocation.
- It omits token, integration, training, review, control, rework, support and governance cost.
- It assumes a productivity gain before evidence exists.
- It does not identify sources, period or uncertainty.

**Example that is useful**

> License, model, token, platform, integration, enablement, review, test, security and governance costs have named sources for the six-week pilot. Production-support cost cannot yet be allocated to eligible work because support effort is not linked to work items. Shared platform cost is available only as a range of $4,000 to $7,000 because the allocation method is pending finance review. These gaps could reverse the apparent economic result. The service owner will establish work-item support attribution, and finance will approve the shared-cost allocation before launch. No savings, productivity, value or expansion claim may be made until the total-cost view is reviewed and accepted.

**Why this is useful**

- It names available and missing costs.
- It states the affected period and scope.
- It discloses uncertainty that could change the result.
- It assigns owners and decisions.
- It prevents an unsupported economic claim.

**Minimum specificity required**

Name every material cost category, period, source, allocation method, actual or estimate status, uncertainty, excluded cost, owner and financial review requirement.

**Evidence expected**

Cost-source inventory, provider or system records, labor and support attribution, allocation method, cost ceiling, finance review and limitations.

**Scenario response**

Material cost is incomplete. Production-support cost is unavailable, and shared platform cost has not been allocated. License, model, token, integration, enablement, review, test, security and governance sources are identified.

**Immediate interpretation**

**Hard stop for economic and expansion claims: Total material cost is unknown**

The missing support and shared-platform costs could change the economic result. The organization cannot claim savings, productivity, positive net value, expansion eligibility or standardization.

**What may continue**

- Readiness remediation
- Cost-source and allocation work
- Establishing an enforced cost ceiling
- After the human stop path is demonstrated, a separately approved learning-only exercise within the cost ceiling and with no value claim

**What must stop**

- Economic-benefit or savings claim
- Productivity claim
- Outcome-evaluated pilot launch under the current evidence plan
- Expansion or standardization decision based on net value

**Required remediation**

Establish production-support attribution, approve the shared-cost allocation, record included and excluded costs and obtain accountable finance review.

### Decision unit 10: Independent review

**Question**

Will someone other than the people performing or sponsoring the work challenge the evidence and result?

**Why it matters**

The people who want the pilot to succeed or who produced the work cannot be the only people deciding whether the evidence is complete and the result is favorable.

**Accountable owner**

Enterprise AI adoption lead

**Required participants**

Measurement owner, finance owner, affected category owners and independent reviewer

**Guidance for answering**

Name a qualified reviewer who did not perform the AI-assisted work and does not own the pilot's success claim. Define which evidence and calculations the reviewer will challenge.

**Structured response choices**

- Named and accepted
- Named but acceptance pending
- Not yet established
- Conflict of interest unresolved
- Unknown

**Written question**

What will the independent reviewer examine?

**Example that is not sufficient**

> The architecture review board will review the pilot results.

**Why this is not sufficient**

- It names a group rather than an accountable reviewer.
- It does not define independence.
- It does not identify evidence or calculations reviewed.
- It does not include adverse evidence or claim limits.

**Example that is useful**

> A principal measurement analyst who is not assigned to the pilot will reproduce the eligible cohort, baseline comparison, downstream effort and total-cost calculation. The analyst will review excluded work, missing evidence, confounders, adverse results and the proposed claim language. Security, quality, service and finance owners will separately decide within their categories. The enterprise AI adoption lead will preserve dissent in the final report.

**Why this is useful**

- It names an independent role and exact work.
- It defines reproducible review.
- It preserves specialized decisions.
- It includes adverse evidence and dissent.

**Minimum specificity required**

Name the reviewer, independence basis, evidence and calculations reviewed, category-owner participation, dissent handling and review date.

**Evidence expected**

Reviewer assignment, conflict check, accepted review scope and later reviewer disposition.

**Scenario response**

Named and accepted. The independent review follows the useful response above.

Independent challenge is planned and accepted. This favorable condition does not cancel open hard stops or convert missing evidence into a favorable result.

**What may continue**

Readiness remediation and evidence design.

**What must stop**
