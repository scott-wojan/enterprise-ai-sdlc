# Anchor 1 Selection Review

## Status

Desktop and mobile structures accepted on 11 August 2026 for the public orientation moment.

These remain low-fidelity structural wireframes. Approval does not cover final typography, color, branding, component styling, detailed interaction behavior, prototype behavior or production implementation.

## Represented moment

This review covers a first-time visitor who:

- has arrived under pressure to establish an enterprise AI adoption approach;
- does not yet understand the product or its method;
- has not created an adoption workspace;
- has not selected an initiative or supplied organizational information;
- needs to determine whether the product applies and where to begin.

## First desktop attempt and correction

The first desktop attempt was rejected because it behaved like a dense product-documentation landing page. It exposed the audience, decision questions, starting paths, operating expectations and capability detail in one viewport.

[Rejected dense desktop attempt](explorations/anchor-1-desktop-rejected-dense.png)

The correction applied progressive disclosure. The public home now answers only:

1. What is this?
2. Why might I need it?
3. Is it meant for someone like me?
4. Where should I start?

Detailed questions, roles, evidence rules, adoption stages and methodology belong on linked guidance pages.

## Product-owner acceptance

On 11 August 2026, the product owner confirmed that the revised desktop structure provides enough orientation without overwhelming a first-time visitor.

The product owner subsequently approved the mobile adaptation.

## Accepted content order

1. minimal product header;
2. public and vendor-neutral context;
3. concise product purpose;
4. concise statement of the enterprise adoption problem;
5. concise explanation of what the playbook provides;
6. one audience statement;
7. two differentiated starting paths;
8. a compact boundary statement.

## Starting-path distinction

### Understand the adoption journey

For a reader who needs a shared approach before assessing a specific initiative. The reader may explore guidance without creating a workspace.

### Assess a specific initiative

For a reader who already has a named use case, team, repository or proposed pilot. Choosing this path begins an adoption workspace for that initiative.

## What must remain outside the public orientation page

- detailed decision questions;
- a role directory;
- adoption stages and operating-model detail;
- evidence dimensions;
- controls and internal identifiers;
- feature and capability lists;
- workbook inputs;
- initiative state;
- readiness progress;
- blockers and decisions;
- organizational forms.

## What this selection validates

- immediate product orientation;
- concise explanation of the enterprise problem;
- audience relevance without a role directory;
- progressive disclosure;
- clear distinction between public guidance and the adoption workspace;
- clear differentiation between the two starting paths;
- preservation of the same meaning on desktop and mobile.

## What this selection does not decide

- the complete public-guidance navigation;
- content of the adoption-journey guidance pages;
- workspace creation and persistence;
- detailed accessibility behavior;
- final visual design;
- working prototype or production implementation.

## Remaining validation

Detailed accessibility annotations and representative first-time-user comprehension testing remain required before Gate 6.4 can close.

The next structural target is Anchor 4, Completed-pilot evaluation. It must separate the evidence conclusion from the later authority and leadership investment decisions.
