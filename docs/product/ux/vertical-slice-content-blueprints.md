# Gate 6.3 Vertical-Slice Content Blueprints

## Status

Proposed for product-owner approval under [Gate 6.3](https://github.com/scott-wojan/enterprise-ai-sdlc/issues/151).

These blueprints apply the proposed content priority and interaction sequence to the sixteen representative views required for the Gate 6 vertical slice.

They define purpose, content order, interaction and boundaries. They are not wireframes and do not prescribe columns, navigation controls, visual styling or components.

## Blueprint rules

Every blueprint states:

- one primary purpose;
- first content;
- primary reading sequence;
- interaction;
- supporting detail;
- material content that cannot be hidden;
- next destination.

Source content remains in the approved Gate 5 experiences and Gate 4 models. These blueprints select and sequence that content. They do not replace it.

## 1. Public Home opening and starting choices

### Primary purpose

Help a technology or delivery leader recognize that the product addresses the enterprise adoption problem and choose an informed next destination without encountering a form.

### First content

**Heading**

Decide where AI belongs in software delivery and how to adopt it responsibly.

**Opening meaning**

The reader may be under leadership pressure to adopt AI or may be trying to replace disconnected experiments with an orderly, governed and evidence-based approach. The product helps decide appropriate work, accountability, access, validation, risk, evidence and investment across the complete SDLC.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Reader situation and product purpose.
2. Challenges a tool rollout cannot resolve.
3. Decisions and outputs the product produces.
4. Complete journey preview from initial alignment through operation.
5. Complete SDLC coverage.
6. Cross-functional participants.
7. Tangible-value and stopping discipline.
8. Starting choices.

### Starting-choice interaction

Ask: **What do you need to do now?**

Choices include Understand, Explore an SDLC activity, Assess readiness, Select a use case, Design an increment, Review an active pilot, Evaluate value, Plan expansion and Resume.

After selection, show:

- decision being prepared;
- expected output;
- accountable owner;
- participants;
- prerequisites;
- useful evidence;
- missing prerequisites;
- public or workspace next action.

### Supporting detail

Expanded Why guidance, role pages, SDLC activity pages, methodology governance and repository source.

### Must not be hidden

- full-SDLC and total-cost standard;
- stopping and remediation as valid outcomes;
- pilot as one increment, not the whole journey;
- no workspace or organizational data before informed Apply.

### Next destination

Public guidance or Apply the Playbook for the selected need.

## 2. Adoption Journey readiness entry

### Primary purpose

Explain readiness as an accountable decision about one proposed scope, not a score or checklist.

### First content

**Stage 2: Assess readiness and establish baselines**

**Decision**

Is the proposed scope ready, restricted, in need of remediation or not ready?

### Primary reading sequence

1. Stage purpose and decision.
2. Why ownership, enforceable stop authority, engineering conditions, evidence and total cost matter.
3. Inputs and prerequisites.
4. Required owners and participants.
5. Conditions that support proceeding.
6. Material conditions that block or restrict.
7. Expected readiness output.
8. Relationship to pilot design and later evaluation.
9. Apply action.

### Interaction

The reader may inspect relevant readiness topics and related SDLC guidance. Selecting **Apply this readiness guidance** carries the Stage 2 intent into Apply.

No readiness response is collected publicly.

### Supporting detail

Organization, engineering, context, control, measurement and team readiness guidance.

### Must not be hidden

- no readiness percentage;
- favorable conditions cannot cancel hard stops;
- meaningful baseline, downstream evidence and total cost are required for an outcome-evaluated pilot;
- missing evidence remains an explicit gap.

### Next destination

Apply the Playbook with readiness preselected.

## 3. Apply the Playbook informed transition

### Primary purpose

Explain the organizational decision and requirements before creating or opening initiative work.

### First content

**Selected starting point: Assess readiness**

**Decision being prepared**

Determine whether the exact proposed work has the ownership, stop authority, engineering conditions, evidence and cost visibility needed for real AI-assisted delivery.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Selected need and decision.
2. Expected readiness output.
3. Exact scope type.
4. Accountable owner and participants.
5. Evidence to gather.
6. Known prerequisites.
7. Consequence of missing prerequisites.
8. Information protection notice.
9. No-authority and provisional-state notice.
10. Begin or Resume action.

### Interaction

The person may:

- begin a named initiative;
- choose an existing initiative;
- return to public guidance;
- change the starting point.

The platform implementation for identity and storage remains undecided.

### Supporting detail

Links to readiness guidance, role responsibilities, evidence principles and protected evidence instructions.

### Must not be hidden

- creating a workspace does not declare readiness;
- do not enter secrets or unnecessary sensitive content;
- the exact decision and output;
- missing prerequisites and their effect.

### Next destination

The current readiness decision area inside the named initiative.

## 4. Initiative Overview in blocked readiness

### Primary purpose

Show the current governing readiness position and route the person to the most consequential unresolved work.

### First content

**Recommendation: Blocked. Do not launch.**

**Exact scope**

Account Services delivery team, Service A authentication defect corrections, proposed six-week Bounded Change pilot through 14 days of production support.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Blocked recommendation.
2. Current Foundation posture and Blocked gate decision.
3. Three active hard stops:
   - stop path not demonstrated;
   - downstream support effects cannot be observed;
   - total material cost is unknown.
4. What may continue.
5. What must stop.
6. Next decision and owner.
7. Remediation owners and dates.
8. Favorable conditions.
9. Source decisions and output.

### Interaction

Primary actions route directly to:

- demonstrate stop path;
- establish support attribution;
- complete total-cost view;
- open readiness decision;
- open leadership briefing.

There is no resume percentage or next step.

### Supporting detail

Verified favorable ownership, safe reporting, outcome, baseline and independent review.

### Must not be hidden

- exact blocked actions;
- current permission;
- all three hard stops;
- remediation does not restore authority;
- value has not been evaluated.

### Next destination

The selected hard stop, remediation or material readiness decision.

## 5. Material readiness decision unit

### Primary purpose

Help required participants determine whether one material readiness condition is demonstrated for the exact proposed pilot.

### First content

**Decision**

Can an authorized human stop the proposed work and enforce the stop without relying on the AI system to cooperate?

**Current consequence**

Pilot launch is blocked because the documented stop path has not been demonstrated.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Decision and exact scope.
2. Current Foundation posture and blocked launch.
3. What may continue and what must stop.
4. Why enforceable human stop authority matters.
5. Accountable owner and required participants.
6. Response choices.
7. Written-response guidance.
8. Evidence expected.
9. Current response and evidence.
10. Interpret action and result.
11. Hard stop and remediation.
12. Accountable reassessment.

### Interaction

Available actions:

- Save draft
- Add evidence reference
- Interpret response
- Inspect hard stop
- Prepare remediation
- Request review when eligible

No action grants launch authority.

### Supporting detail

Detailed rule, prior response versions, related control and decision history.

### Must not be hidden

- current blocked consequence;
- exact identity and access scope;
- operational demonstration requirement;
- what may continue and what must stop;
- new decision required after remediation.

### Next destination

Hard-stop remediation or the consolidated readiness decision.

## 6. Written response with useful and insufficient examples

### Primary purpose

Help a person write a specific, evidence-ready answer without inventing certainty.

### First content

**Question**

Describe the human and technical stop path for this exact scope.

**Minimum specificity**

Name declarers, identity and access affected, enforcement actions, verification, evidence preserved, latest exercise date, observed result and restart owner.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Why the response matters.
2. Insufficient example.
3. Why it fails.
4. Useful example.
5. Why it works.
6. Evidence expected.
7. Written input.
8. Evidence reference.
9. Save and Interpret actions.

### Interaction

Examples remain accessible while typing. Save draft preserves the answer without interpretation.

Unknown and Not yet established remain valid. Selecting either shows the evidence gap and likely restrictive consequence before the person interprets.

### Supporting detail

Additional examples, definitions of stop authority and evidence-protection instructions.

### Must not be hidden

- why each example is useful or insufficient;
- minimum specificity;
- evidence expected;
- honest uncertainty path;
- no prefilled organizational answer.

### Next destination

Interpretation in the same material decision unit.

## 7. Evidence reference and evidence-gap handling

### Primary purpose

Connect a decision to a protected, reviewable source and explain the consequence when material evidence is unavailable or limited.

### First content

**Evidence requirement**

Provide a dated stop-path exercise using the proposed pilot identity and repository boundary.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Condition supported.
2. Expected source and owner.
3. Exact scope and date.
4. Protected reference instructions.
5. Current evidence status.
6. Material limitation.
7. Claim or action affected.
8. What may continue.
9. What must stop.
10. Evidence owner, reviewer and required next source.

### Interaction

The person may:

- Add evidence reference
- Save draft reference
- Submit for review
- Review evidence for declared use
- Return to the triggering decision

Verification is a separate reviewer action.

### Supporting detail

Provenance, transformation, retention, access classification and version history.

### Must not be hidden

- evidence status;
- limitation;
- decision use;
- exact gap consequence;
- owner and next evidence action.

### Next destination

The material decision, remediation or evidence review.

## 8. Immediate hard-stop consequence

### Primary purpose

Tell the person immediately that a named action is prohibited and make the safe response clear.

### First content

**Blocked: pilot launch cannot proceed**

The organization has not demonstrated that an authorized human can stop the proposed pilot identity and prevent further repository action.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Stopped action and exact scope.
2. Why the condition governs.
3. What may continue.
4. What must stop.
5. Immediate containment.
6. Required corrective action.
7. Owner and due date.
8. Completion evidence and verifier.
9. New reassessment decision.

### Interaction

After Interpret response, focus moves to this consequence.

Actions:

- Open remediation
- Return to response
- Open source evidence
- Close proposal without proceeding

No dismiss action removes the hard stop.

### Supporting detail

Rule identifier, detailed control mapping and history.

### Must not be hidden

Everything through reassessment decision remains in the primary path.

### Next destination

The linked remediation.

## 9. Hard-stop remediation and reassessment

### Primary purpose

Guide corrective work while preserving the current restriction and the need for a new human decision.

### First content

**Finding**

No demonstrated human stop path.

**Current restriction**

All real AI-assisted work for the assessed scope is blocked.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Corrective action.
2. Accountable owner and due date.
3. Dependencies.
4. Completion evidence.
5. Verifier.
6. Current status.
7. Reassessment owner.
8. Explicit authority statement.

### Interaction

The owner may record progress and submit evidence for verification.

The verifier may record Verified or Rejected.

After Verified, show:

**Verified for reassessment. The prior blocked decision still governs.**

Then offer **Begin reassessment**.

### Supporting detail

Progress log, prior evidence, rejected submissions and decision history.

### Must not be hidden

- current restriction;
- verifier independence;
- verification does not restore authority;
- new decision required.

### Next destination

A new material readiness decision unit.

## 11. Explicit later-scenario transition

### Primary purpose

Let a validator inspect the completed-pilot evaluation without implying that clicking advanced blocked readiness into a completed pilot.

### First content

**This is a separate later representative scenario**

The blocked readiness state will not change.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Statement that the scenario is fictional and separate.
2. Earlier hard stops were hypothetically remediated and verified.
3. A new readiness and launch decision occurred.
4. The bounded pilot ran and ended.
5. The downstream observation period completed.
6. An evidence snapshot was frozen.
7. The prior authority ended.
8. No expansion authority exists.

### Interaction

Primary action: **Inspect the later completed-pilot scenario**

Secondary action: **Return to blocked readiness**

### Supporting detail

Hypothetical chronology and source assumptions.

### Must not be hidden

- no real state changes;
- remediation and launch required human decisions;
- this is not automatic completion.

### Next destination

Pilot Evaluation in the later scenario.

## 12. Completed-pilot evaluation

### Primary purpose

Determine what the completed pilot demonstrated across the entire affected SDLC and total cost.

### First content

**Decision**

Should the exact Service A use case stop, restrict, remediate, continue or become eligible for one bounded expansion?

**Current state**

Pilot completed, prior Bounded Change period ended, evidence decision pending and no expansion authority.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Exact scope and frozen snapshot.
2. Comparability and limitations.
3. Evidence completeness.
4. Complete-system conclusion.
5. Product outcome.
6. Delivery flow and total human effort.
7. Quality and maintainability.
8. Security and control.
9. Release and operations.
10. Team experience.
11. Total economics.
12. Adverse evidence and dissent.
13. Claim limits.
14. Separate authority decision.
15. Separate investment decision.

### Interaction

Reviewers inspect and disposition each dimension. The evidence conclusion becomes available only after material evidence and dissent are addressed.

### Supporting detail

Calculations, cohort definitions, source versions and extended reviewer notes.

### Must not be hidden

- local faster implementation;
- worse complete flow;
- quality, operations, participant and cost deterioration;
- limitations;
- no composite score.

### Next destination

Evidence-conclusion decision.

## 13. Negative net-value evidence conclusion

### Primary purpose

Record what the complete evidence allows the organization to claim without changing authority automatically.

### First content

**Evidence conclusion: Negative net value demonstrated**

### Primary reading sequence

1. Exact evaluation scope.
2. Complete rationale.
3. Implementation effort decreased.
4. Total human effort and elapsed time increased.
5. Review, correction, defects and churn deteriorated.
6. Support and change-failure evidence deteriorated.
7. Participant burden increased.
8. Total cost increased from $3,400 to $4,130 per eligible change.
9. Product outcome did not materially improve.
10. Security remained within boundary with increased review burden.
11. Limitations and dissent.
12. Claim prohibitions.
13. Explicit statement that authority has not yet changed.

### Interaction

The accountable evidence owner records the conclusion and rationale against the frozen snapshot.

The next action is **Decide permitted AI participation**.

### Supporting detail

Dimension records, calculation detail and reviewer challenge.

### Must not be hidden

- favorable local observation;
- complete negative result;
- total cost;
- limitations;
- no productivity, savings, success, positive-value or expansion claim.

### Next destination

Accountable authority decision.

## 14. Accountable restriction to Assisted Work

### Primary purpose

Record the human decision that removes state-changing AI authority while preserving explicitly allowed lower-risk work.

### First content

**Decision required**

What AI participation is permitted for Service A authentication defect corrections now?

**Recommendation**

Restrict from Bounded Change to Assisted Work. Do not expand.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Exact scope.
2. Negative net-value conclusion.
3. Category-owner constraints.
4. Authority removed.
5. Activity retained.
6. What may continue.
7. What must stop.
8. Remediation option.
9. Evidence and approvals required for reconsideration.
10. Owner and effective period.

### Interaction

The accountable adoption owner records the restriction after required category dispositions.

Unsupported choices, including continued Bounded Change and expansion, are unavailable with reasons.

### Supporting detail

Prior pilot authority, full decision trace and category memoranda.

### Must not be hidden

- evidence conclusion does not make this decision automatically;
- exact state-changing actions removed;
- Assisted Work is not a positive-value conclusion;
- prior approval cannot be reused.

### Next destination

Leadership investment decision.

## 15. Leadership investment decision

### Primary purpose

Help the executive sponsor decide whether to fund bounded remediation or stop further investment without overriding category authority.

### First content

**Decision required now**

Choose whether to fund bounded remediation under Assisted Work or stop the Service A authentication-defect use case.

**Recommended action**

Maintain the restriction. Fund remediation only as a bounded learning investment with an explicit cost ceiling and no guarantee of return.

### Primary reading sequence

1. Exact scope and current Assisted Work authority.
2. Negative net-value and total-cost result.
3. Category-owner decisions.
4. Material limitations and dissent.
5. Option: fund remediation.
6. Consequence, cost boundary, owners and next evidence.
7. Option: stop the use case.
8. Consequence and preserved learning.
9. Delay consequence.
10. Unavailable options and why.
11. Rationale, owners and review date.

### Interaction

The authorized leader records Fund remediation, Stop further investment or Delay. The current restriction remains regardless.

### Supporting detail

Tangible-value assessment, category decisions and remediation plans.

### Must not be hidden

- leadership cannot select continued Bounded Change or expansion;
- funding does not restore authority;
- total cost and adverse evidence;
- exact next proof required.

### Next destination

Recorded investment decision and derived leadership output.

## Cross-blueprint continuity checks

### Public to workspace

The decision, output, participants and evidence described in public Apply match the workspace decision that opens.

### Response to consequence

The hard stop appears in the same material decision context immediately after interpretation.

### Consequence to remediation

The hard stop and remediation share the exact scope, stopped action, owner, evidence and reassessment decision.

### Readiness to later scenario

The later transition explains the hypothetical human decisions and operating period. It does not alter blocked readiness.

### Evaluation to authority

The evidence conclusion and authority restriction are separate records and separate interactions.

### Authority to investment

The executive sponsor receives only options permitted by current category decisions and authority.

### Source to report

Every output derives from frozen decisions, evidence, risks and limitations. Reports do not own source state.

## Blueprint approval questions

1. Does every view have one clear primary purpose?
2. Does every first content block state the real decision, consequence or reader situation?
3. Are examples and evidence available at the exact point of response?
4. Does the hard stop interrupt the task clearly?
5. Is remediation useful without implying automatic restart?
6. Does the later scenario explain the missing organizational time and decisions?
7. Does evaluation preserve the faster implementation result while reaching the negative complete-system conclusion?
8. Are authority and investment separate after evaluation?
9. Is the leadership output actionable without exposing a raw record dump?
10. Are these blueprints specific enough for Gate 6.4 wireframes without deciding layout?
