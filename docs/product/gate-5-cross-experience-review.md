# Gate 5 Cross-Experience Review

## Status and purpose

This review completes the fourth review step required by [Gate 5](https://github.com/scott-wojan/enterprise-ai-sdlc/issues/140). It evaluates the three approved end-user content experiences as one connected product path before Gate 5 receives final approval.

The reviewed sources are:

- [Experience 1: Understand the Purpose and Choose a Starting Point](experience-1-orientation.md)
- [Experience 2: Determine Whether Responsible Pilot Work Can Begin](experience-2-pilot-readiness.md)
- [Experience 3: Evaluate a Pilot and Present the Leadership Decision](experience-3-pilot-evaluation.md)

This review does not approve visual design, software architecture, platform, persistence, deployment or production changes.

## Review outcome

The three experiences form a coherent vertical product story and are ready for final Gate 5 approval.

They demonstrate that the approved method can be translated into end-user content that:

- explains the adoption problem before asking for information;
- lets a reader choose a relevant starting point without entering a wizard;
- applies structured responses and evidence to a real enterprise decision;
- identifies hard stops immediately;
- distinguishes permitted work from prohibited work;
- creates owned remediation and requires verification;
- evaluates value across the complete affected delivery system and total cost;
- preserves adverse and dissenting evidence;
- restricts AI authority when the complete result is negative;
- produces team and leadership outputs that support an accountable decision.

The source packages contain the complete content model. They must not be rendered as three continuous pages. Gate 6 must validate the smaller connected experience defined in this review.

## Connected product story

### Experience 1: Understand and choose

The reader arrives after being asked to establish an enterprise approach to AI-assisted software delivery.

The product first explains:

- why it exists;
- who it is for;
- which enterprise decisions it supports;
- why tool adoption is not the complete problem;
- the full adoption journey;
- the complete SDLC scope;
- who participates;
- how evidence, risk and stopping protect the organization.

The reader then chooses **Assess readiness** and reviews the decision, output, likely participants, prerequisites and evidence before entering organizational information.

### Experience 2: Determine whether work may begin

The adoption workspace opens for one exact representative scope. It does not show a page counter or questionnaire score.

The workspace demonstrates favorable sponsorship, ownership, baseline and independent-review conditions. It also identifies that:

- the human stop path has not been demonstrated;
- production-support effects cannot be attributed;
- total material cost is incomplete.

The workspace immediately blocks real AI-assisted work and outcome-evaluated launch. It prevents value, savings, productivity and expansion claims, creates three owned remediation items and produces a readiness report and leadership briefing.

### Explicit time and decision boundary

Experience 3 is a later representative state. It does not follow because the user clicked a next button.

Between Experiences 2 and 3, the representative organization:

1. demonstrates the human stop path;
2. establishes production-support attribution;
3. completes the total-cost method;
4. verifies all three remediations;
5. records a new readiness and launch decision;
6. runs the bounded pilot;
7. completes downstream observation;
8. freezes a new evidence snapshot.

Gate 6 must state this transition directly. It must not simulate elapsed enterprise work, verification or approval through page navigation.

### Experience 3: Evaluate and decide

The product evaluates the completed pilot against its frozen evidence snapshot.

Implementation became faster. Total human effort, elapsed time, review, correction, quality, maintainability, operations, participant experience and total cost became worse. Security remained within its approved boundary but required more review effort. Product and service outcomes did not materially improve.

The complete evidence produces **negative net value demonstrated**. Authorized owners restrict the scope from Bounded Change to Assisted Work. No expansion or favorable productivity, savings, success or positive-value claim is permitted.

Leadership receives two available investment choices:

- fund bounded remediation under Assisted Work; or
- stop the use case.

## Terminology review

### Terms used consistently

The three experiences use the following shared terms with one meaning:

- **Current AI participation:** the kind of AI use currently allowed for the exact scope.
- **Foundation:** preparation and testing before real AI-assisted delivery is authorized.
- **Assisted Work:** AI may analyze, explain, recommend and draft while humans perform state-changing actions.
- **Bounded Change:** AI may make a change only within an approved technical and organizational boundary.
- **Current decision:** what accountable people have authorized now.
- **Evidence conclusion:** what the observed evidence supports as a claim.
- **Hard stop:** a condition that blocks a named action or requires current work to halt.
- **Remediation:** owned corrective work with required completion evidence and verification.
- **Reassessment:** a new decision after required evidence is verified.
- **Tangible value:** the complete result after downstream effects, adverse outcomes and total material cost are included.
- **Learning-only exercise:** a separately approved activity that cannot support value, productivity, savings or expansion claims.

Detailed method identifiers remain outside the primary reader-facing path.

### Example-label correction

The content packages originally used slightly different headings for written examples. They now use one reader-facing pattern:

- Example that is not sufficient
- Why this is not sufficient
- Example that is useful
- Why this is useful
- Minimum specificity required

No other material terminology contradiction was found.

## Decision-state consistency

### Experience 2

- Current AI participation: Foundation
- Work status: Preparing evidence
- Gate decision: Blocked
- Evidence conclusion: Value not evaluated
- Proposed Bounded Change: Not approved

These states are consistent. A blocked launch does not create a negative-value conclusion because evaluative work has not begun.

### Experience 3 before evaluation

- Current AI participation: Bounded Change for the completed pilot period
- Work status: Pilot completed, decision pending
- Evidence conclusion: Decision pending
- Expansion authority: None

### Experience 3 after accountable interpretation and category decisions

- Current AI participation: Assisted Work
- Work status: Bounded Change stopped; leadership investment decision pending
- Gate decision: Remediation required
- Operating consequence: Restricted to Assisted Work
- Evidence conclusion: Negative net value demonstrated
- Expansion eligibility: Not eligible

These states are consistent. The evidence conclusion does not change authority by itself. The enterprise AI adoption lead and affected category owners record the restriction. The executive sponsor then decides whether to fund remediation or stop the use case.

## Risk and claim consistency

### Experience 2

Favorable sponsorship and baseline conditions do not cancel the three hard stops. The content correctly distinguishes:

- the universal stop-authority condition, which blocks all real AI-assisted work;
- missing downstream evidence, which blocks an outcome-evaluated launch, value conclusion and expansion claim under the approved plan;
- incomplete total cost, which blocks economic, productivity, value and expansion claims.

A learning-only exercise remains unavailable until the universal stop-authority condition is cleared. It then requires a separate approval, boundary, cost ceiling and claim prohibition.

### Experience 3

The faster implementation result remains visible. It does not cancel:

- higher total human effort;
- longer total elapsed time;
- greater review and correction;
- more escaped defects and churn;
- higher production-support effort;
- worse participant experience;
- higher total cost;
- no material product-outcome improvement.

The scenario correctly selects **negative net value demonstrated**, not **mixed result**. The local implementation improvement occurred inside an overall system that became materially worse.

The restriction to Assisted Work is narrower than stopping all AI use and broader than a warning. It removes state-changing AI authority while retaining lower-risk analysis and drafting.

## Role and decision consistency

The experiences preserve one accountable human for every consequential decision.

- The executive sponsor owns enterprise intent, investment and the decision to fund or stop remediation.
- The enterprise AI adoption lead coordinates the adoption decision and records posture and authority restrictions.
- Product owns the intended product or business outcome.
- Delivery owns complete flow, capacity and participant operating conditions.
- Quality owns quality and verification disposition.
- Security owns security and boundary disposition.
- Release and service owners decide production and operational acceptability.
- Measurement owns the comparison method and evidence package.
- Finance owns total-cost and economic disposition.
- Participant representatives preserve sustainable operating conditions and adverse evidence.
- Independent reviewers challenge evidence and calculations.

Role and SDLC views are secondary lenses. They must link to these same decisions and records rather than create separate versions.

## Cognitive-load review

### Source packages are not pages

The three source packages contain 22,000 words of exact copy, examples, decision logic, outputs and author notes. Exposing them directly would recreate the density problem this recovery is intended to solve.

Gate 6 must use the source packages as a content system and present only the material needed for the current user task.

### Public orientation requirements

The first view must show:

- the direct heading;
- the real situation that brings the reader to the product;
- what the product helps decide;
- actions that continue to explanation or journey guidance.

It must not show:

- a workbook;
- a role selector;
- an operating model;
- a readiness question;
- a progress count;
- a giant navigation sidebar;
- the full nine-stage detail at once.

Home may preview the journey, SDLC coverage, participants and evidence discipline. Detailed content belongs at stable linked destinations.

### Workspace decision requirements

The workspace must focus on one material decision unit at a time while keeping these facts visible:

- exact scope;
- current AI participation;
- current decision;
- hard stops and restrictions;
- what may continue;
- what must stop;
- next decision and owner.

This is not a wizard. The interface must not show “step 4 of 19,” a page-completion percentage or a next button that implies organizational progress.

Users must be able to move among decision areas directly. The current decision unit may use a clear action such as **Save response and interpret**, followed by the immediate consequence in the same context.

### Written-response guidance requirements

Before a person types, the experience must provide:

- why the question matters;
- who owns the decision;
- an example that is not sufficient;
- why it is not sufficient;
- an example that is useful;
- why it is useful;
- minimum specificity;
- evidence expected;
- an honest unknown or not-yet-established path when applicable.

Examples may use progressive disclosure after the reader has seen that help is available. They must remain accessible before and while writing. The product must not replace them with placeholder text inside an empty field.

### Evidence requirements

The primary view should ask for a protected evidence reference, source owner, scope, period and status. Detailed provenance, retention and transformation fields may appear when the evidence is added or reviewed. They should not be repeated beside every public explanation.

### Consequence requirements

When a response triggers a hard stop, the consequence appears immediately in the same decision context. The user must not open a report or finish other questions to discover it.

The first consequence view shows:

- direct condition;
- exact scope stopped;
- what may continue;
- what must stop;
- owner and corrective action;
- evidence required for reassessment.

Detailed method references and history remain available as secondary information.

### Report requirements

Reports begin with the decision required and recommended action. They use readable sections, not a wide matrix or a dump of captured fields.

The leadership view shows:

- decision required now;
- recommended action;
- exact scope;
- complete-SDLC and total-cost result;
- governing risks and restrictions;
- what may continue and what must stop;
- available options and consequences;
- owners and dates.

### Mobile requirements

The vertical slice must work in one-column reading order. No essential meaning may depend on hover, a wide table, a permanently open sidebar or horizontal scrolling.

## Gate 6 vertical-slice content boundary

Gate 6 should implement the smallest connected experience that tests every essential product behavior.

### Included public orientation

- First visible heading and opening explanation
- Who the product is for
- Enterprise challenge preview
- Complete-journey preview with links or focused detail
- Starting-point choices
- **Assess readiness** selection
- Informed transition into the workspace

### Included readiness workspace

- Exact representative scope
- Current posture
- Favorable sponsorship and ownership summary
- Human stop-authority decision unit
- Downstream support-evidence decision unit
- Total-cost decision unit
- Immediate hard-stop consequences
- What may continue and what must stop
- Three remediation records
- Readiness output
- Leadership briefing

The prototype does not need to present every favorable readiness question as a full interaction. Favorable conditions may be shown as linked, verified scenario evidence so the user can focus on the three governing decisions.

### Included transition to completed-pilot evaluation

The prototype states that the earlier remediations were later verified, a new launch decision occurred, the pilot ran and a new evidence snapshot now exists.

The transition must be labeled as a later scenario state. It must not appear as automated completion of real organizational work.

### Included pilot evaluation

- Frozen snapshot and comparability summary
- Complete-SDLC evidence overview
- Separate dimension results
- Local implementation improvement
- Negative complete-system result
- Evidence-conclusion decision
- Accountable restriction to Assisted Work
- What may continue and what must stop
- Leadership choice between remediation and stopping
- Tangible-value assessment and leadership briefing

### Included prototype state and export

The prototype must let a validator:

- enter or select representative responses;
- see interpretations change from those responses;
- inspect the resulting risks, restrictions and remediation;
- move between the readiness and evaluation scenario states;
- produce a readable leadership output;
- preserve or export the validation result in a clearly provisional form.

The Gate 6 implementation may use disposable prototype state. It must not predetermine the Gate 7 platform, identity, storage or collaboration decision.

### Excluded from Gate 6

- Complete nine-stage public site
- Every readiness decision unit
- Every role page
- Every SDLC activity page
- Complete control library
- Authentication and enterprise identity
- Multiuser collaboration
- Production evidence storage
- Final report distribution controls
- Final hosting and deployment decision
- Production-site replacement

## Remaining approval decision

Gate 5 may close only when the product owner approves:

1. this cross-experience review;
2. the explicit time boundary between blocked readiness and completed-pilot evaluation;
3. the cognitive-load requirements;
4. the Gate 6 vertical-slice content boundary;
5. the three experiences as one connected content system.

After that approval, Gate 6 may begin. Gate 6 must produce a working prototype and validation plan. It must not begin a complete-site rebuild.
